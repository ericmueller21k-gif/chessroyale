# Fair play: simulated cheaters and honest players

Generated by `packages/sim/scripts/fairplay-sim.ts` (5,000 honest players per strength, 1,250 cheaters per type, 5 Crowd 50 v 50 matches each). Every pick is summarized and scored by the server's own code (`core/fairplay.ts`) with the thresholds in `settings.ts` (`FAIRPLAY`). DECISIONS.md, "Fair play", has the summary.

## How it's simulated

- **Positions:** `fairplay-bank.ts` played 30 Crowd games from the starting position the way a crowd does (50 honest players pick, the most popular move is played), 1440 positions in all. Each was analyzed once: the judges' search (the browser engine, 250k nodes, top 8, plus every other move anyone might pick), Stockfish's own MultiPV-4 lines at depths 1-11, and a strong engine (the full network, 1M nodes, top 3), which stands in for both a cheater's engine app and our deep re-check.
- **Honest players** pick the way Stockfish's limited strength (UCI_Elo) does, emulated exactly from those lines (`src/skill.ts`), at 1200 to 2700, with ±100 of form from match to match. The crowd around them is honest players of N(1500, 350). Think time: lognormal, longer on harder moves (3.5 s + 9 s × the share of 1500 players who'd miss the move). A look-away on 2% of moves.
- **Cheaters** copy the engine with 3-12 s delays whatever the move. Their engine agrees with our deep re-check's best 85% of the time (else they play our second choice): two strong engines, depths or versions don't always agree, so this is deliberately not 100%. Types: every move; 20-40% of moves (per player) at 1500 and 1900; only in hard positions; only when their own move would lose 5+ points; and a blend of the engine's 1st, 2nd and 3rd choices (50/30/20%). "Same phone": a look-away on 90% of engine moves.
- **Eddie-like** (Nov 11, after the live demo): every pick another engine's top move, here the judges' own search's (250k nodes), which is the strong engine's exact best about as often as the live demo's Stockfish 19 was our 17.1's (74%).
- **Strong people** (Nov 11): Maia-3 at 2200, 2400, 2500 and 2600 (`fairplay-maia.ts`: its moves in every bank position, any it would play that the judges hadn't scored scored the same way), either its likeliest move every time ("likeliest", as the demo's Strong Sam) or drawn as people vary (temperature 1, top-p 0.95). The likeliest move is the same every time in the same position, and the bank has 30 games, so its rows move in clusters.
- **A match:** each player is on a team of 50 and picks on its team's plies: a cut after picks 10, 15, 19, 23, 26, 29 (the team-final schedule, half of each cut per team), to the final eight. The final's few moves aren't simulated (fewer counted moves than real finalists get: conservative).
- **The deep re-check** runs on any match that scored 2+ or of a player already flagged, before their next match. The bank has no deep scores, so a pick other than the strong engine's best loses what the judges see between the two, either way round (Nov 11; before, a pick the judges preferred lost nothing).
- **"Few people":** the same matches with fewer than 8 other people picking (bots fill the lobby), so there are no crowd rates and only strength-based chances apply.

## What this can't tell

- Real people aren't Stockfish at a lower strength: their mistakes are more human (missed tactics), and how often a strong person matches the engine may differ. The evidence model is fitted on the emulated players; refit it from real games (`honestChance`) once there are enough flagged-and-cleared matches, and keep auto-bans conservative until then.
- How often a real cheater's engine agrees with our deep re-check is assumed (85% for the copy-the-engine types; the live demo's 74% for Eddie-like). Lower agreement means slower bans, never more false ones.
- The deep re-check's loss for a pick other than its best is the judges' gap between the two (the bank has no deep scores), so who counts as near the best is an estimate: two engines as close as the demo's (Stockfish 19 and 17.1, both at about 2M nodes) disagree on closer moves than the judges' quick search and the strong engine here.
- Timing and look-aways are modeled, not measured; they add few points and never ban on their own.
- A cheater using the engine on a fifth to two fifths of moves plays like a stronger honest player: no move-by-move statistic can tell them apart without far more matches. Reports and a strength far above their own history are what catch them.
