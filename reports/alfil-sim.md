# Alfil's calibration (Oct 17, 2026)

Alfil's two powers (docs/decisions/2026-10-17-alfil-rules.md) in the raid sim: a Solo raid of 50 bots against him at
his raid strength, through the match runner (the rage meter, both powers, his steps weighed on the sim's engine), one
game per seed, seeds 1 to 13 and 31 to 43, paired with the pack's runs (`rf1`, `rf2`, Oct 15) and today's reference
(`ref1`). Generated by `packages/sim/scripts/boss-sim.ts raid alfil 1 <seed> 0 <tag> [patch]`; the rows are in
`reports/boss-sim/raid-<tag>.jsonl`. Score: the crowd's result per battle (a win 1, a draw ½).

**The result: the fusion is his move (V1, adopted).** With the elephant's landing as his whole turn, the crowd scores
**0.23** (± 0.08, 26 battles), inside the pack's spread. As first built (the fusion at the start of his turn, then his
move) he won all 26 battles. The call and its reasons are in the decision file.

**Not repeatable seed for seed.** A seed fixes the match's own random source, but the boss's moves are Stockfish's at a
set strength (UCI_Elo), which Stockfish draws from the clock (reports/engine-reader.md). So the same seed plays a
different battle each run: on the same code (aff97c6), seed 1 ran 17 crowd moves once and 20 the next; V4's seeds 1 to
13 scored 0.08 where `f8a`, the same rule on the same seeds, scored 0.23. Compare runs by their totals and standard
errors (about ± 0.08 at 26 battles), never battle by battle.

**The counter (`counter: 1` in the patch):** the bots pick their moves through the judge, which doesn't know the
fusion, so on their own they never take a marked pawn on purpose. With `counter: 1` the whole crowd plays the best
capture of a marked pawn when it costs at most a pawn against its best move: a crowd that knows the rule.

**His strength in these runs:** the bots' tier is 1600; at his offset (−250) he plays at 1350 (Stockfish's floor is
1320) and slips to a random legal move 25% of the time, as every boss this far under its tier does. An offset of −400
puts him on the floor itself (1320) with the same slips: no lever at this tier.

### What it says

- **The fusion as his move brings him into the pack:** 0.23 (V1), between Sawyer's 0.15 and Big Boy's 0.35 on today's
  code, in 32 crowd moves. It changes nothing Eric decided (two elephants, the cast, glow and fusion flow); the other
  variants change more and score no better for the crowd: the less advanced square 0.15, the first cast at his 6th turn
  as well 0.21, the first cast at his 8th turn with the fusion as first built 0.08.
- **As first built, he was out of the pack:** every battle his (26 of 26), in 25 crowd moves, the shortest of any
  boss. The Slam alone puts him mid-pack (0.50, 48 moves): Tusks is the whole difference.
- **Why one fusion decided it, as first built:** each is a bishop for two pawns, and Stockfish calls that a won game.
  (The probes in this paragraph and the Slam's, 39 positions and 16 lifts in the decision file, came from one-off
  scripts that aren't committed.)
  Its verdict (200,000 nodes) from the starting position with the side ahead to move: one pawn up (White's a2 gone),
  0.79; two pawns up (a2 and b2 gone), 1.000; Black's a7 and b7 made a bishop on a7, 1.000 for Black, and 0 for White
  with White to move. A probe on positions from engine play (seeds of the opening book, his side to move every third
  move; 21 balanced ones): the median fusion took his expected score from about 0.5 to 0.999, the measure's ceiling
  (+7.3 pawns in the bounce's log-odds), and only 4% of their 292 fusions read under 1.5 pawns. The sheet's "about 1
  point" is the material count; to an engine it's a won game.
- **The cap tried first didn't bound it, it switched it off:** with the fusion's gain held to 0.5 to 1.5 pawns, two
  fusions in three failed with no counter (67%, s1; 69% with the pair picked within the cap, s2), and the ones that
  came were in games already decided (every recorded gain 0: both readings at the ceiling). That 0.35 came from a power
  that mostly didn't happen, so it went (the decision file).
- **As first built, the numbers the sheet leaves to me didn't move it:** a cast every 8 turns instead of 5 (u8a, 0 of 5:
  his games end before a later cast matters); his offset (no lever at this tier, above). The counter happens (10 of 64
  casts countered) but a marked pawn is often out of reach.
- **Two numbers Eric set, measured before V1 (13 battles each), not needed now:** the first cast at his 8th turn, 0.23
  (`f8a`; 0.08 when re-run at 26 battles as V4); one elephant, 0.08 (`m1a`). The capped version: 0.35, but the fusion
  fails two times in three.

  the review round, a Slam that lifts nothing keeps the meter and comes again on his next turn, and a check no longer

- **The measure is coarse, as the fusion's:** the played-out line mostly reads 0 (the game's verdict unchanged, often
  already decided) or a big swing either way. The lift picked was in the band (0.25 to 1.5 pawns) for 1 of 43 lifts in
  V1, 0 of 25 in u5, 5 of 36 in e2 and 8 of 61 in s1; of every lift weighed, 1 of 182, 0 of 119, 7 of 136 and 9 of 236.
  What the cap does in practice: never a lift that turns a balanced game into a lost one for the crowd. The engine, thinking he's
  five points up, often plays into the pieces' return: lifts that leave the crowd better off (9 of 36 alone) are
  allowed, as Eric expected ("you can plan around it, and he can't").
- **Down early** (he'd have a mate in one only because they're up): 2 of 25 lifts as first built, none of 43 in V1.

## The landing guard (Oct 17, after Eric played him)

On his move just before the Slam's pieces land, his pick and his four best moves are judged with them down, and a pick
past the blunder guard there gives way to the best (docs/decisions/2026-10-17-alfil-sees-the-landing.md). The same
raid sim, V1's seeds, `counter: 1`; "before" is today's main (the review round's rules, not re-simulated until now).

  V1). Above the 0.08 line for leaving the pack.
- **The guard judged 41 moves** (every lift's landing but one, in a battle that ended first) and swapped 10; it searched

- **Its cost on a quiet machine** (`slamGuardNodes` 30,000 a position, 150,000 at most): 16 positions from the opening
