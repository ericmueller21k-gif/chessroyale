# Review fixes (PR #863, the Saw Swap judge)

Each finding, what was done, and why. Decision note updated (`docs/decisions/2026-11-21-saw-swap-judge.md`: calls 4 to 7, "Review
fixes, the calls", Cost); `docs/areas/engine.md`, "Judging past a power", rewritten to match.

## 1. Bug (fair-play trust): swap numbers could go unchecked

**Rule chosen:** a look past the swap counts only where two devices gave it exactly alike: two judges agreeing (`agreed`) or two of
three (`majority`). Everywhere else a board stands on one device's look, those moves are scored as plain chess, the pre-PR behavior,
at no engine-server cost:

- a lone judge with the engine server on (`settleTask`'s single path: `plainOf`);
- a dispute's verdict or fallback, unless the other judge's look is exactly alike (`lookTrusted`, all or nothing: the bots' picks
  follow every number of the look, so a per-move share would leave bots picking with numbers nobody vouched for; `sharedSwaps` removed);
- a newer app's look beside an older app's board (finding 3: `agreedBoard` uses the board without the look).

The bots go plain too: every report with a look also scores the bots' picks as plain chess has them (`runQuickJob`), and the board
carries them (`JudgedBoard.plainBots`; `baseBoard` requires them scored), so the plain board always holds together and a lone liar
can't steer the bots either. The spot check and the late-answer comparison still see the judge's full board (look included).

Why this rule: it's the simplest that never trusts an unchecked number, and it needs nothing new from the engine server (which can't
see the board after the swap). Calls (in the decision note): a lone judge with **no** engine server keeps its look (nothing checks a
lone judge's numbers then, plain or not, and its own re-check was worked out with the look); the host scoring alone and solo keep it
(the host's word already stands for every number, unvouched). A dispute only about the look with no third device ends with no strike
(the verdict can't tell who lied; the liar gains only plain chess for that turn); with a third device two of three decide and the odd
one is struck, as before.

## 2. Bug (players see it): online hints and the eval bar used plain chess

The boss view now carries the moves the judge looks past with their boards (`NetBoss.powers.swap`, from the runner's `judgeAfter`;
missing otherwise and from older servers). `NetMatch.withSwap` folds them in for the power-up hints and the eval bar exactly as solo
does (`swapScores` over the cached top-moves search, then `swapIn`). On the fork test the hint now leads with the quiet move, the fork
at 0.2, the bar at 0.6; without the look (an older server, or the swap used) plain chess as before. On a turn the judge reads as plain
(finding 1's fallbacks) the hints still show the look's value: advice, as the brief allows.

## 3. Nit: an older app's report during a deploy window got an honest old app struck

`boardsAgree` now compares two boards as plain chess when exactly one has a look (the older app abstains): its board and the newer
one's plain board (`plainOf`) match, so no disagreement, no referee, no strike; `agreedBoard` then uses the older app's board (the
newer look is one device's word). The same in the referee's two-of-three match and the devices' re-checks. The Crowd's early bot
plan is checked against either judge's bots with or without the look, so a plan made either way is never struck. Decision call 4
reworded.

## 4. Nit: the re-check measured close calls against the plain best

New `recheckView` / `recheckAnchor` (runner-tools.ts), used by `recheckTargets`, `applyRecheck` and `recheckCloseCalls` through an
optional `swapped` argument: the re-check works on the final numbers (`swapJudged`'s), so losses are measured from the best the game
charges from; it is anchored on the judge's best or, when that's a swap move, the best of the other moves; swap moves are never
targets nor searched. Every path passes the look it will apply: solo/the runner, the host's own re-check (`net.ts`), the host's
engine-server re-check (`lobby-do.ts` → `serverRecheck`, the host's look filtered to the job's moves), the judges' deep check
(`deepTargets`, `judgedBoard`), and the lobby's server re-checks (`accept`, `deepFallback`, `serverScored`). Without a look every
function is exactly as before. "$0" corrected: the engine server never searches a board after the swap nor (now) a swap move, but its
re-check of close calls (one search a board, as before) can fall on somewhat different turns; not measured.

## 5. Nit: bots couldn't pick a move the judge may call best

`swapIn` now adds a swap move that isn't in the list when its score after the swap is above the list's best (the judge can name such
a move its best: `swapJudged`); one below stays out, as any move outside the top moves. Tested: with one top move (the fork, 0.2 after
the swap) and Nd6+ outside it at 0.95 after the swap, the bot plays Nd6+ and the report scores it.

## 6. Nit: a fair-play comment overstated its rule

`runner.ts` (resolveBoard's fair-play comment) now says what the code does: the turn counts when the engine's best keeps its own score
in the game's numbers; for the race that makes it the race's best (the old guarantee); for the Saw Swap it means the engine's best
isn't a swap move, and fair play's record (plain chess's numbers) can then differ from the game's for the swap moves either way,
including charging more than the round's loss for one the swap helps. Decision call 5 says the same.
