===== COPY FROM HERE =====

You're writing original dialogue for HunChess, a chess game for phones and computers. Players fight cartoon boss
characters at chess. Sometimes one player fights a boss alone; sometimes a whole crowd of players votes on each move
together, so "you" in a line should work for one player or a crowd. Each boss is a chess opponent with two special
powers: a passive power that comes every few turns, and an ultimate that comes when its rage meter fills. The players
have a champion, the God King, who stands by the board and can step in (he strikes the boss or plays a move).
Players also walk around a town square where the bosses and their little kid sidekicks live. A chat box in the top
corner shows what they say now and then, and players can play the kids at chess tables in the park. Players can
also ride vehicles called rides around town; every boss has a ride made in its style.

Lines show in a small speech bubble during battle, so they're short and punchy. The tone is a funny, friendly
cartoon for all ages: teasing, never cruel.

WHAT I NEED

RULES FOR EVERY LINE
- Battle lines: 70 characters at most, including spaces. Town and kid lines: 60 at most. One sentence or two
  short ones. Use … for trailing off.
- Original. No famous catchphrases, song lyrics, movie quotes, brands or trademarks.
- Never name, quote or hint at a real person. That includes chess players and openings named after people,
  which is why open.other never says the opening's name.
- Never use the words Soviet, Russian or Grinch. Hollow never says Santa. Ginger never says "snap".
- There's a secret boss in the game. Never mention any boss, character or place that isn't in this prompt.
- American English spelling.
- Stay in character. Each boss sounds like itself, never like a generic villain. Go easy on its pet words, tics
  and sound effects: at most about one line in five, never two in a row.
- Talk about powers the way a player sees them on the board, never with numbers or rules text.
- Fair play: never hint at a good move for the player, and never give away anything hidden (which of Smudgy's
  pawns is the rook, where Puddles' seeds are, what's in Hollow's dark).
- Don't repeat a line the boss already says, or another line you've written. Vary the shape of the lines, not
  just the words.
- Pronouns as given in each entry.
