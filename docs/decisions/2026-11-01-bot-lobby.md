# The bots as a lobby of 800 to 1500 players, Stockfish-style (Nov 1, 2026)

**Eric (Nov 1):** the bots of a bots-only Crowd 50 v 50 play "very stockfishy" and should play off Maia, at varied
strengths; later the same day, after finishing about 20th with the engine's help on every move: "a player making the
engine's best moves should finish 1st or 2nd"; the bots' ratings "mostly about 800 to 1500, some range above and below",
like a real lobby of whoever is online; speed decides between Maia and Stockfish-style picks.

**What changed.**
- Every bot has a rating fixed by its name (`botRatingOf`, `BOT_LOBBY.ratings` in settings.ts: a bell curve, mean 1150,
  sd 250, 600 to 2200, steps of 100): about 89% between 800 and 1500, about 5% each side. The same bot is the same
  player every match. `botRoster` seats them everywhere a lobby is filled (Crowd and Classic, solo and online; Squads;
  a Solo raid; Recluse's phase 2 mates).
- Each rating plays at a temperature (`botSkillForRating`, `BOT_LOBBY.skillAt`) that gives away, in a Crowd game, the
  points a move Maia gives away at that rating (`reports/maia-calibration.md`'s per-move table: 1200 → 5.2 points a
  move, 1500 → 4.4, 900 → 6.8). The temperatures' losses were measured in the Crowd itself (the probe in
  `reports/crowd-bots.md`); a temperature can't give away more than about 7.3 a move (it picks among the judge's top 8),
  so 600 sits at the loosest that still counts (128).
- The judge, the bosses' brains, duels, the town's kids (Maia, at their fixed ratings: `town/world.ts`, `duel.ts`), the
  God King and the test helpers are unchanged. `?weakbots=1` still floors every bot at 8 and ranks people first.

**Maia or Stockfish-style: Stockfish-style.** This branch first wired Maia for the bots (commit 7379b0f3: one ask per
rating among the bots in a position, a 2.5 s budget, Stockfish-style when it didn't answer). Measured: 73 ms an ask
on this machine, 10 ratings among 50 bots, so 0.74 s of Maia every team turn; 197 ms an ask in a full match with the
judge busy beside it. A phone (the CPU slowed 4x, as for the bosses' Maia) needs about 3 s a turn, past the budget, on
the device that is also judging, after a 40 MB download for every Crowd player, with Maia's memory beside Stockfish (what
stopped Eric's iPhone loading Maia in October). Stockfish-style picks cost nothing (the judge's top moves are already
searched) and play the same on every device. The Maia wiring is taken out (simpler; it can come back from that commit).

**Test 3** (`packages/sim/scripts/crowd-bots-sim.ts`, an engine-best player among 99 bots): 1st or 2nd in 9 of 20
before (places down to 45th, 22nd, 20th, 17th), 7 of 10 with the first try, 19 of 20 after (one 3rd). What's left is the
judge changing its mind (a deeper re-check naming another best move), not the bots: `reports/crowd-bots.md`.
