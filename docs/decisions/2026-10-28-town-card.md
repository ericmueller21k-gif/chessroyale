# The town's person card, redone from Eric's mockup (Oct 28, 2026)

Phone and computer (Eric judged the size on a phone; he named no platform otherwise, so it's both). Builds on the town bots
(`2026-10-27-town-bots.md`) and their sidekicks (`2026-10-28-sidekicks-in-town.md`). The area page, `docs/areas/town-square.md`
("Cards", "A person's card"), is updated in the same PR.

## Eric's words (Oct 28), with his mockup

His mockup, made with ChatGPT: a dark card with the sidekick's picture on the left in a blue ring, "Woolly (bot)", "Alfil's
sidekick", "Rating 2200" with small gold bars, and a gold "▶ Play" on the right.

> "this is what it looks like when you tap on someone. You can click on their profile picture to view their profile if they're
> not a bot. … Obviously, it got the icon wrong, but that's fine. You see what it was going with. … it doesn't need that
> little triangle next to it, and it should be that same size. Like, the sizing that it has here is good, at least for mobile.
> … make it play, and then just like a … typical … challenge icon where it's like kind of two swords or whatever, like a basic
> little challenge icon sitting to the left of play instead of that triangle"

The director's calls (the brief): the card stays where it is now (over the person, its tail pointing at them, clamped on screen),
because the mockup anchors it to a chip only as the picture was made; the other cards get the same dark look and keep their
content; "Tables are full" moves out of the button to small gray text under the rating; "Play"'s name, the dialog's label and
every test hook stay.

## What it is

- **Every card is dark**: near-black `#20212a`, a thin warm-gold border (`#d9bb8c`, 1.5 px), rounded corners, a tail with the same
  border, white words, gold primary buttons (a `#fdda7e` to `#f3b957` gradient, dark words), plain buttons dark gray with white
  words, greyed buttons dimmer and flat, "Coming soon" gray. The boss, home, table, Colosseum and "Leave the town square?" cards
  keep their words, buttons and layout. The colors are the mockup's, sampled from it. The chat's menu keeps the light `.town-btn`
  (the dark styles are scoped to `.town-card`).
- **A person's card is one row, one size: 232 × 60 px**, on a phone and a computer alike (a phone is 390 px wide: it's 59% of
  it; Eric's rule, "nothing hidden, nothing shifts": "(bot)", the sidekick's line and "Tables are full" don't resize it, nor does
  a pawn's missing line):
  - **The picture** on the left, a 40 px round with a 3 px ring: a sidekick's face (`spriteFace`) in a **blue** ring; a pawn's own
    picture (the pawn as it stands in the square, its crate items on, cut to what's drawn) in an **ivory** ring; a real player's
    profile icon (`UserIcon`), also in ivory. It's made once as the card opens, never in the frame loop.
  - **The words**: the name in bold white, " (bot)" in gray after a bot's; under it a sidekick's line ("Alfil's sidekick"); then
    "Rating 2200" with a small gold signal-bars icon; then a row for the note, which says "Tables are full" when they are.
  - **Play**, gold, on the right, with a plain crossed-swords icon (inline SVG in the button's text color, `aria-hidden`) to
    the left of the word, in place of the mockup's triangle. Its name is still "Play". Greyed and not tappable when the tables
    are full.
- **Tapping the picture**: a real player's is a button, "View <name>'s profile", and opens their profile, the same one a tap on
  a name opens elsewhere (`openProfile({ uid })`, over the town; Back, and the town and its card are as they were). A bot's is a
  plain box: no button, no button cursor, no focus, and a tap does nothing.
- **No real players walk in the town yet.** A person gets the player's card when it has an account id (`Person.uid`, with
  `icon` and `rating`). Only an e2e test player has one: the `__townPlayer(uid, name, icon?, rating?)` hook (test switches only)
  stands one beside you, and `__townAt("player", uid)` says where to tap it. `__townFull(on)` (test switches only) puts someone
  unseen on one stool of every table, so a bot's Play greys.
- **Where it sits** is as before: over the person's name tag, tail pointing at them, clamped on screen (`placeCard`, unchanged).

## The size, from the mockup's name tags

The mockup's pixels mean nothing by themselves, so the game's own name tags are the ruler (the tags are drawn 11 to 12 px high
in the game: `tag size = clamp(11 × k / kf, 8, 12)`). Its gold "you" tag, which sits at the camera's focus (so 11 px, a 19 px pill
at the 3x of a phone, 57 device pixels), is 70 pixels high in the mockup; so the mockup is 1.23 times a phone at 3x, and a
tag-sized 57 device pixels is 0.81 of the mockup's. At that scale the mockup's card is about **187 × 53 px**, its ring 40, its
name about 13 px bold, its other lines about 9 px, its Play about 52 × 24. (`out/compare-mockup-vs-ours-day.png` in the PR's stills
puts the two side by side at one scale.)

- **The type is the mockup's**: the name 13 px bold, " (bot)" 12 px in weight 500, the lines 9.5 px (the mockup's are about 9),
  Play 12 px, the ring 40 px with a 3 px ring.
- **The card is wider and taller than the mockup's, 232 × 60 against about 187 × 53,** for two reasons, both Eric's rule: it's
  one size whoever it is, so its width has to fit the longest name and line of any bot, which the mockup's "Woolly (bot)" and
  "Alfil's sidekick" don't ("Chub & Chirp's sidekick" at 9.5 px is 105 px of its 111 px text column; "Tempo Tess (bot)" is 103);
  and it reserves a fourth row for "Tables are full", which the mockup doesn't have. An e2e check measures every bot's label, line
  and rating in the card's own fonts (with 4 px to spare); every one fits whole. If Eric would rather the card hugged its content
  (about 190 px for Woolly, 232 at most), it's `width: 232px` in `.town-card.town-person` and the grid column; the cost is that
  cards of different names differ in width.
- **Tap targets**: the Play button is drawn 28 px high, as the mockup's is (it's 24 px there), with a 44 px tap area (a
  transparent `::before`, over the card's own padding; nothing moves). A real player's picture is a 40 px button.

## Calls

- **The rings.** A sidekick's is blue (`#4575fa`, the mockup's), to match the blue name tags the chat helper is adding to the
  sidekicks (same blue? That helper's constant isn't in `main` yet: if theirs differs, `--ring` of `.town-pic.sidekick` is the one
  place). A pawn's and a real player's are **ivory** (`#eadfc6`): a pawn's tag is white, a player's will be too, ivory reads on the dark
  card at 12:1 and goes with the gold border; blue reads at 3.95:1 (a picture needs 3:1). A pawn and a player look different
  inside the ring (a pawn on a white disc; a player's drawn icon), and a player's has no "(bot)".
- **A pawn's picture** is its own pawn as dressed in the square (the crate items it wears, its tint), cut to what's drawn and set
  in the middle of a square with room round it (a pawn's wide foot would be cut at the round's corners). Made from the art the
  renderer already holds (`botArt`) when the card opens.
- **The note row is always there** (nothing hidden, nothing shifts): a pawn's card has an empty line row, and every card an empty note
  row until the tables are full. It's under the rating, as the director said; I didn't put it in the sidekick line's place for a
  pawn, because a sidekick's card would then need both and the two kinds would differ.
- **A real player's Play** is greyed, with "Coming soon" in the note row: challenging a player isn't built, and the card shows it
  rather than hiding it (the other cards' pattern). Nobody sees this card in the real town yet.
- **A long name** in a real player's card ends in an ellipsis (their profile shows it whole); the dialog's label has it all.
  A bot's name never does (the check above).
- **The swords** are two blades crossing, each with a crossguard, a grip and a pommel, one path drawn twice and turned 45 degrees
  each way, heavy enough to read at 1x on a computer. Single color (the button's text), 14 px.
- **The border is 1.5 px**, which is 1 px on a computer's 1x screen (a browser rounds a border down to a device pixel) and 1.5 on a
  phone: thin on both, as in the mockup.
- **The tail** is a square turned on its corner, bordered on the two sides that make the point, over the card's own border, so
  the gold line runs into it. Same 7 px point as before.
- **Contrast** (computed from the page's own colors by `e2e/town.spec.ts`, and here from the colors): the name white on the card
  16.0:1, " (bot)" `#a5a4aa` 6.5:1, the sidekick's line `#d6d8df` 11.2:1, the rating `#eceef2` 13.8:1, "Tables are full" `#aeb2bd`
  7.6:1, "Coming soon" `#a9adb9` 7.1:1, Play's dark `#1a1d2b` on the gold's two ends 12.4:1 and 9.5:1, a greyed button's `#9a9eab` on
  its `#272831` 5.5:1, white on a plain button 13.5:1; the gold bars 8.3:1, the ivory ring 12.1:1 and the blue ring 3.95:1 (pictures:
  3:1).

## Frame rate (Eric's rule: the card is HTML over the canvas; nothing in the frame loop)

Method (`docs/rules/eric.md`, as `2026-10-26-town-square-slice-2.md` and `2026-10-28-bots-rotation.md`): headless Chromium, a phone
390 × 844 at 3x and a computer 1440 × 900, the town's default view by day, 4 s to warm, then 5 s counted in the page; the medians of
the runs taken in turn, main (origin/main at #399) measured twice as the noise control, the two builds served as built files, the
machine quiet (under the shared e2e lock, nothing else of mine running). Main has the old card open in "C"; this branch has the new card
open in "D": a sidekick's card, standing (a tap elsewhere closes a card, so it can't be open while walking).

- **Nothing was added to the frame loop.** The card is HTML over the canvas; its picture is made once as the card opens (a
  tap, not a frame); no drawing code was touched (no `render.ts`).
- **What the first round found, and the fix.** With the card open the branch read a little under main (phone 58.8 against 59.1 fps,
  computer 55.6 against 56.7, about 0.5 ms of main thread a frame), inside the noise between two runs of one build (0.0 and 1.0 fps)
  but the same way in both shapes. The cause was an old habit that a card with more parts shows: `placeCard` wrote the card's
  `transform` and its `--ax` (which every part of the card inherits, so the browser works the card's styles out again) every frame,
  even over someone standing still. It now writes them only when they move (as the chips do), so a card over someone still writes
  nothing. Placement is the same to 0.05 px.
- **After it** (6 runs each, medians; the new card is cheaper than the old one with it open, and level with main otherwise):

| fps (main-thread ms a frame) | phone | computer |
|---|---|---|
| main, card open (twice) | 58.7 (14.45), 58.5 (14.62) | 55.0 (18.18), 56.0 (17.75) |
| **this branch, card open** (twice) | **59.5** (14.40), **59.1** (14.32) | **58.3** (16.71), **57.3** (17.03) |

- **Walking, no card open** (no code of the card runs; main in "A", the branch in "B"; two earlier rounds, 4 and 5 runs, the branch
  as built before the placement change, which can't matter with no card open): phone, main 56.3, 57.0 and 56.8 fps against the
  branch's 55.4 and 56.2; computer, main 51.2, 51.2 and 51.1 against 51.8 and 52.2. Level, within the noise.
**Frame rate: no slower than main.**
