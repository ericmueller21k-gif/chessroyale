# Rides: Riding Chirp and Moon's Ride in the town (Oct 31, 2026)

Eric (Oct 31), of the two rides drawn the day before: "send me… a GIF of it running around town with a piece mounted to it… and
then… an idle animation, the usual." Equip one and your pawn rides it round the town square: its idle standing, its move walking.

## What changed

- **A ride slot** (`ride` in crates.ts's `ItemSlot`; `ITEM_SLOTS` lists every slot, and the server's equip check reads it): one ride at a
  time, stored like every slot (D1 `equipped_items`, no new column or table), in your look (`cleanLook` keeps it), in what others see
  (`looksOf`, the public profile). **Riding Chirp** and **Moon's Ride**, both Mythic, with a color, a purity and shine like every item.
- **No source yet**: in no crate, shop or drop (the crate comes later). Admins own them as admin copies (#430, "Admins own one of every
  item", merged into this branch: red, 100%, `admin:riding-chirp`, worn in the ride slot like any item, and seen by others); nothing here
  touches how ownership is counted. The tests give the test account rides straight in the local database (`giveItems`, e2e/helpers.ts,
  as the e2e helpers already write emails there), and one has an admin wear `admin:riding-chirp` and ride it in the town.
- **The drawings** were copied as drawn from `claude/boss-art` (components/rides/; only their import lines and a few `!` changed). The paint
  they share with every item (color, blotches, shine, `Region`) moved out of Items.tsx, word for word, into `item-paint.tsx`, so the rides
  and Items.tsx both import it without a loop between them.
- **The locker**: a Ride tab; wearing one, both preview pawns ride it, its idle playing. **The profiles**: your profile's pawn, a public
  profile's pawn and your Wearing rows. The tile shows the ride with a white pawn on it.
- **The town square**: anyone wearing a ride rides it (you; a bot or a test player as others see them; real players later), Canvas 2D and
  WebGL alike (the rider is one picture, drawn as a pawn's is).

## Calls made

- **Pre-rendered, not drawn in the town** (Eric's frame-rate rule): each ride's frames are baked in every color into one lossless WebP
  a ride and color (`scripts/bake-rides.ts`, as the sidekicks' sheets are; bit for bit the drawing, checked as it bakes), 2.0 MB for
  the 18 sheets (5.0 MB as PNGs). They're **left out of the service worker's precache** (by name and extension): the town fetches only
  the sheet a rider in the square wears (one, 50 to 175 KB), decodes it off the page's thread (`createImageBitmap`), composes the
  rider's frames straight from it (the ride's back, the rider's pawn on its seat, its front), one frame an animation frame, and lets it
  go. A frame draws one picture per rider, as for a pawn: nothing new per frame, nothing allocated.
- **Memory**: riders who look the same (ride, color, the pawn's side, tint and items) share one set of frames (about 4 MB); a set is
  freed when no one rides on it, and all of them when the town closes, so none follows you into a game. A sheet that fails to come is
  forgotten, so a later rider fetches it again. With ten riders in view, each its own set, on WebGL: no atlas page started again
  (`__townPaint().resets` 0, phone and computer), and the sheets all let go once composed.
- **Taps**: a rider takes taps only on its pawn's own box across, as on foot, not over its ride's reach (up to 1.46 squares from its
  foot), so a tap on the square beside you walks there. Riders are drawn from 1.5 squares off the screen's sides (pawns from 1), so a
  ride never pops in.
- **What the sheets hang on**: their hash (the test that keeps them fresh) covers the ride's drawing, the paint's fills and lines
  (`item-paint.tsx`) and the colors, not the blotches (`blotches.ts`) or anything else, so tweaking another item never asks for a bake.
- **The corner cut**: in the town a ride shows its **color**, not its purity's blotches or its shine (both belong to each copy, and the
  sheets are per color; a copy's own frames would mean drawing the SVG in the town). The locker and the profiles show the copy exactly.
- **The walk sets the pace** (the brief: "adjust the art's frame timing to the pace, not the pace to the art"): a ride's move plays one
  cycle a step, its frames' times scaled to the step (90 ms yours, 200 ms a bot's; the art's 650 and 640 ms became shares of a step). The
  art's own move across the square (`dx`) isn't used: the walk moves the figure, evenly. Its hop (`dy`, the grasshopper's) lifts the figure
  instead of the pawn's hobble, its shadows shrinking with it. The idle plays on its own times.
- **Size and footing**: the pawn keeps its size (the ride at the pawn's scale: the art's 100 units are the pawn's box), and the ride
  stands on the square's middle (the grasshopper's feet, the moon's shadow: `TOWN_FEEL.rides`). The moon draws its own shadow, so the town
  draws none under it; the grasshopper gets the town's, a little wider than a pawn's.
- **Name tags**: over the highest of the ride and its pawn standing (a hat, the moon's stars), and steady through the hop, as a
  sidekick's tag is through its walk (the hop is 11 times a second for you). Tags and the boss's bubble are drawn after every figure, so a
  ride never covers one; a card sits over the tag, as ever.
- **Sitting at a park table** the pawn sits on the stool as before, without its ride; standing up, it's on its ride again.
- **Mounting moves nothing else**: in the town only the rider's own figure (and its tag) changes, and until the frames are ready the person
  walks on foot. In the locker and the profiles the rider is fitted into the pawn's own box (the pawn about 80% of its size there), so no
  box grows or moves (e2e: the preview's boxes before and after).
- **Facing**: mirrored when walking left, as pawns are (the art faces right). Walking straight up or down keeps the last facing.
- **Where rides don't show** (the pawn alone, as before; `Avatar` draws a ride only when asked): a match's screens (votes, the cut, the
  crowd, the duel's strips, the boss dock and stage, auto chess), the Colosseum's crowd, home's hero pawn and the menu's ring, the queue's
  and Squads' cards, the town's person card picture and the bot game's strip, and the public profile's Equipped Cosmetics row (four tiles
  at most; its View all lists the ride).

## Frame rate (eric.md's method)

Headless (this machine has no GPU: WebGL is SwiftShader's software rendering, so its numbers say little), a phone (iPhone 13,
390 x 844) and a computer (1440 x 900), `?crowd=50` by day, 5 s walking up and down the promenade, the medians of four runs taken in
turn, one pass; `main` is 42a7c1e0 (after the review's fixes). fps; JS = the main thread's script time a frame (CDP
`ScriptDuration`); task = all its work a frame.

Level with main on both backends, on foot or riding (inside the run-to-run spread). (The first measurement, before the review's
fixes, was the same: Canvas 2D 48.5 / 48.7 fps on the phone, main / riding; WebGL 13.8 / 13.0.)

**When a sheet arrives and a rider mounts** (a long-task observer, four runs each): a rider on Moon's Ride in a color not yet fetched
walks in while you stand. Before the review's fixes the phone had 1 to 4 long tasks (58 to 91 ms, 62 to 272 ms in all: the sheet
decoded on the page's thread, cut into 36 canvases, and the first frames composed, in one go); after, 0 or 1 (at most 63 ms, in the
browser's own drawing as the rider first shows: no script of ours in it, by a profile). The computer had none either way, and a test
player walking in on foot causes none.
