Phone and computer (Eric described both). Builds on the town chat ([2026-10-28-town-chat.md](2026-10-28-town-chat.md)) and its

## What it is

- **One box, see-through, flush in the map's top left corner** (8 px in, under the header). The lines are one continuous stream
  from the box's top, newest at the bottom; the field "Say something…" is fixed at its foot, a plain line with no box round it
  and **no send button** (the keyboard's Send or Enter only). No backing on the box or on a line: `--chat-back` is an opacity,
  0 now, for Eric to raise later. Light text with a soft dark shadow reads over the sky, the sand and the grass.
- **Every line of the last minute is kept and the box scrolls**: a finger on a phone, the wheel on a computer, the map under it
  not panning or zooming. New lines keep it at its foot unless you've scrolled up; then it stays where you are and a small
  "↓ new" says lines came.
- **A chat icon and a gear at the right end of the "Town square" bar.** The icon's three modes, a tap going to the next:
  **always on** (the default; top bubble green, bottom gray), **new messages only** (top gray, bottom green; the lines show when
  a message comes and fade after `fadeAfterMs`, the field with them), **off** (both gray; no box, no field; the lines, the
  chatter and other players' lines still arrive, so turning it on shows the last minute). Named "Chat: always on", "Chat: new
  messages only", "Chat: off". Remembered on this device. The gear opens "Town settings": the same three as choices, and nothing
  else yet.
- **Taps:** a player's name opens the line menu (Profile / Mute / Report / Remove). In new messages a tap on a line's text brings
  back every line of the last minute until it fades again (Eric's "tap a message and then you see the faded ones"). A tap on the
  box away from any line reaches the map. Press and hold still picks the box up to move it.
- **Folio's house moved two squares east**, from (33, 30) to (35, 30): Eric, "folio's house is like 2 inches from the colosseum
  it's gotta get moved a bit".

**The buttons** (Eric, Oct 28, later, through the director; phone and computer): "remove the 'Back to me' button in town screen
replace with circular focus icon … Move the zoom button down so it's as follows zoom button on left, focus button to right but
are in the bottom right corner." The text label is gone: a round focus button (36 px, as high as the −/+ pair; a ring, four ticks
and a dot in our own inline SVG) sits to the right of the pair in one row in the map's bottom right corner, the same 14 px inset
as before. Its accessible name is still "Back to me" and it recenters the camera as before. The chips, the statue close-up and the
chat box read the buttons' box (`.town-controls`), so they keep clear of the row without changes of their own; the town card was
never kept clear of them (it is drawn under them), and the row, 36 px high and no longer stacked, covers less of it.

## My calls

1. **Sizes kept.** A phone's box is about two thirds of the map's width and a bit under a quarter of its height (249 × 182 px on
   a 390 × 844 phone), a computer's 500 × 340, the font 12/14 px and 14/17 px. Without padding and backings the lines pack
   tighter: about 9 single lines (5 messages of one or two lines) on a phone and 15 on a computer, so "about 5 and 12" holds and
   the mockup's look (5 messages and the field) fits without growing the box over the map. The box keeps one place and size in
   always on and new messages.
2. **Lines start at the box's top, the field stays at its foot.** A quiet box has a gap between its few lines and the field; it
   can't be avoided if the box keeps one size and place (the rule) and the lines are to sit in the corner (Eric). The empty part
   passes taps to the map.
3. **No backing, so the text carries itself.** `--chat-shadow` is four layered soft shadows (a tight black halo twice, a short
   drop, a wide blur); a single drop shadow read poorly over the white clouds and the sand squares. Checked at the brightest and
   darkest spots of the town (the day sky's clouds and the Colosseum's sand; the night sky and its lit gate), stills in the PR.
   One exception: a refusal's reason over the field ("That message isn't allowed.") keeps a small red backing, since it is an
   alert and not a line, and the "↓ new" marker is a small dark pill (a control, not a line).
4. **"The faded ones" in new messages.** Eric asks to tap a message to see the faded ones, which needs some lines to be faded. So
   a burst of messages (everything said before the box next fades) shows only its own lines, and the rest of the minute waits for
   a tap on a line's text: then every line comes back, scrollable, until it fades after `fadeAfterMs` with nothing new (a scroll,
   a touch of a line or a message starts the wait over). The lines stay as they are while it fades, so nothing jumps; the next
   burst shows only itself. A tap on a boss's or kid's line does nothing in always on.
5. **A player's name opens the menu in both modes; their text too in always on.** The brief names the name. Eric's earlier ask
   (via the director, [typing decision](2026-10-28-town-chat-typing.md)) was a tap anywhere on a player's line, and a name is a few
   letters wide, so in always on a tap on the text still opens the menu. In new messages the text is the way to bring the box
   back, so only the name opens it. A name's tap area is a few pixels bigger than the letters.
6. **Native scrolling, not our own.** The box has `pointer-events: none`; each line has `auto` and `touch-action: pan-y`, so a
   finger on a line scrolls the stream with the browser's own momentum and a finger anywhere else on the box is the map's. The
   wheel is caught over the box (when it can scroll) and scrolls it, so it never zooms the map. Pressing and holding a line picks
   the box up as before; while it's carried the browser's pan is called off (a `touchmove` `preventDefault`).
7. **Off is a hidden box, not an empty one.** `display: none`: no box, no field, the edge chips may use its place (Eric's choice
   to turn chat off; nothing else about the town changes). The log keeps filling (the same one `say`, the chatter and the players'
   poll feed), so turning chat on shows the last minute; nothing extra is asked of the server. In new messages the field is
   hidden with the lines (as Eric said); the box and the field are hidden with `visibility` after the fade, so a faded box takes no
   taps and can't be tabbed to.
8. **The icon.** Two bubbles with three dots each, drawn small in plain fills: the lit one is the app's own green (`--good`), the
   gray one a light gray in the light theme and a darker one in the dark, white dots, a thin ring in the button's color between
   them. The gear is the match options' gear. Both are 36 px circles like the back button, so the bar looks the same.
9. **The sheet reuses the match options' look** (`mm-scrim`, `mm-panel`): "Town settings", a small "Chat" heading and three
   radio rows (Always on, New messages, Off) with a tick by the one in use; a tap outside, its X or Escape closes it. **Suggestion
   for Eric: nothing else in it for now.** Sound, and the town's own settings (the Simple map, Oct 25) would be the natural next
   rows; he hasn't named any.
10. **A 4th argument for the test hook:** `__townSay(name, text, kind, agoMs)` says a line said `agoMs` ago, so a test can scroll
    back to a line 50 s old.

## Frame rates (eric.md's method)

Eric's method (`docs/rules/eric.md`; `2026-10-26-town-square-slice-2.md`): headless Chromium, a phone 390 × 844 (pixel ratio 3)
and a computer 1440 × 900, the default view, 5 s walking (a tap ahead every 1.2 s), frames counted in the page, medians, runs taken
in turn. Main measured twice (A and A2: the noise control). A line arrives at 1, 2.5 and 4 s of each run. Three measurements:
4 runs in a fixed order, then 8 and 8 in a different shuffled order each round. All on a shared 4-core machine, which is noisy:
one configuration's runs swung by 5 to 10 fps (main quiet: 41.4 to 53.6 on the phone), and A and A2 medians differed by up to 1.2.

| medians over 20 runs (40 for main) | phone fps | computer fps |
|---|---|---|
| main, nothing said (A + A2, the noise control) | 50.5 | 39.6 |
| main's v1 box full and showing (12 / 5 lines, each on its own dark backing) | 50.2 | 37.9 |
| **this branch, always on, a full 60 s stream (60 lines, scrolling)** | 48.0 | 39.4 |
| this branch, off (box hidden, the stream still arriving) | 49.6 | 37.9 |

By measurement (medians of 4, 8 and 8 runs; the last on the final build): main quiet 52.3 / 50.2 / 50.6 on the phone and 40.5 /
38.2 / 40.0 on the computer; main's v1 box 51.5 / 48.6 / 48.6 and 38.0 / 38.3 / 36.8; this branch always on with 60 lines 47.7 /
47.7 / 50.9 and 38.2 / 40.5 / 38.8; this branch new messages showing 47.7 / 47.7 and 40.6 / 37.1; this branch off 49.4 / 50.0 /
48.3 and 37.9 / 38.2 / 37.6. The town before any chat, 51.3 and 39.2.

What it shows, honestly: on a computer the one see-through box (39.4) is level with main (39.6) and 1.5 fps above the v1 box on
dark backings (37.9; the measure last time read about 2 under main). On a phone the three medians put this branch 2.5 under main
and 2.2 under the v1 box, but the sign flips in the last measurement (50.9 against 50.6 and 48.6), and the box hidden reads 49.6
(it should read the same as main), so the phone's difference is inside this machine's noise and I can't claim it costs less there. A
paired check inside one page (the box shown for 3 s, then `display: none` for 3 s, six times over) read the same: the box's cost
was within 1 fps for v1 and v2 on a phone, and its spread across CSS variants of the same box (one text shadow, none, no mask,
`contain`) was −3.6 to +4.9 fps, which is noise. Nothing is in the town's frame loop: the box is plain DOM updated when a line comes
or goes (a few a minute), the fade is a CSS opacity transition, a scroll is the browser's own, and the chips read one rectangle. The
measured build is the branch before the final merge of main and the buttons' row, neither of which touches the frame loop; the
last measurement was on the final build.

### My calls

1. **The box in new messages keeps its place and its size**, and the stream runs to its foot (the field's strip is the stream's, about
   two more lines). The alternative, a shorter box, would have moved the chips and the bottom edge between modes. "Nothing hidden,
   nothing shifts" holds within every mode and across them.
2. **New messages' icon: the top bubble green, the bottom gray** (Eric left the choice open: "just one for the middle option").
3. **How a name's color is picked: the player's device, each time the town opens, carried by the server.** The brief offered the
   account id plus a per-join value the server already gives, or the room id plus the account id. The server gives no per-join value
   (its `room` id changes only when the live hub restarts: a deploy, or the hub idling out, so a player would keep a color for days),
   and nothing else of theirs reaches other players. So the device picks a number at random when the town opens (`TownFeed.open`:
   a new one every visit, the same for the whole visit), and sends it with every message as `color`; the server checks it's a whole
   number below 32 (`tidyColor`; anything else is dropped, the message still goes) and carries it on the line (`TownLine.color`);
   every reader draws the name in palette[color mod 10]. Everyone sees the same color for that player's visit. A line without one (an
   older app, a test-hook line) gets a steady color from the account (else the name). Cosmetic only: a player could pick their own,
   which harms nothing. This touches the server's town chat (an optional field on the town chat's own endpoint, not the lobby
   protocol), so the PR's e2e runs the chat specs too. Your own name uses the palette like the rest.
4. **The palette: ten colors** (`names.ts`), each a deeper shade for a light sky and a lighter one for night, so a player's color is the
   same color across the switch (same hue, the lightness moved). I sampled the pixels under the box in every sky (day, dawn, dusk,
   night; a phone's and a computer's) and chose the shades by contrast against them: the day shades hold at least 3:1 on about three
   quarters of what lies under the box on a phone (the lime two thirds; the rest is the Colosseum's shadowed brown) and more on a computer;
   the night shades on a little over half (white text on 80 to 95 percent). No two are closer than ΔE 18 (CIELAB), and none is near the
   bosses' gold or the kids' mint. Ten, not twelve: the dark shades of more hues run into each other.
5. **Dawn and dusk count as day.** The tone is night's weight in `skyWeights` against `TOWN_FEEL.chatNightAt` (0.5): it flips in the
   middle of each blend, 5:30 and 19:30. Sampled under the box, black ink reads (3:1) on 89 to 96 percent of the pixels at dawn and day,
   71 to 86 at dusk and 31 to 44 at night; white on 9 to 25 percent at dawn and day, 44 to 49 at dusk and 82 to 94 at night
   (phone 82, computer 94). Dusk's sky and the lit sand are mostly bright, so dusk keeps ink; only night is dark.
6. **The tone is one attribute on the box (`data-tone`), not an inline variable.** The brief said one CSS variable; two palettes (and
   the gray and the gold and mint) need selectors, and an attribute gives plain computed `rgb()` colors where a `color-mix()` of a
   variable would give browser-dependent ones. It is read by the chat screen itself about once a minute (`skyCheckMs`) from `?sky=`
   or the clock through `skyWeights`, and a re-draw happens only when it flips. Nothing is added to the town's frame loop (the town's
   own sky code is untouched). The test hook is the existing `?sky=day|dawn|dusk|night`, and Playwright's clock for the flip.
7. **Grays and the two kinds' names by tone:** the private lines' gray `#4f5561` by day and `#c8ccd2` by night; a boss's gold `#7a5600`
   by day and `#ffd479` (as before) by night; a kid's mint `#0a5f36` by day and `#9be8c0` (as before) by night. The placeholder, the
   greyed field and the scroll bar use the softer ink (68 % by day, 80 % white by night).
8. **Kept as they were, on purpose:** the "↓ new" marker's small dark pill (a control that shows only while you are scrolled up with
   lines below; Eric's close-up has none) and the dashed outline while the box is carried (without its tint). Either goes in a line if
   Eric wants it.
9. **`docs/rules/eric.md` gets one bullet**, the field in always on only, with its guard test, as that file asks of a new standing rule.
