# Ranked 1 v 1: the core (Oct 16, 2026)

Eric's top priority (Oct 16): "1 v 1 · Classic chess", normal chess between two signed-in players, one tap to queue,
an on-board time-control vote, League of Legends ranks over a hidden rating. This file records the calls made building
it; how it works is in [docs/areas/ranked.md](../areas/ranked.md), and every number is in `RANKED_1V1`
(`packages/core/src/settings.ts`). The home screen's RANKED row, the versus art and the first-time hint in Crowd and
Squads are another helper's (they call `startRanked1v1()`, read `ranked1v1Label()` and reuse `VoteHint`).

- **New:** a game object of its own (`DuelGame`, one per game, migration v6) rather than the lobby object: a two-player

  game run them, so the bot game can't drift from the ranked one.

  1 v 1 is plain Fischer (minutes + increment), as Eric's table says, so its five controls are `RANKED_1V1.controls`.

## The time-control vote

- **The rule** (`votedControl`): the controls in order 1+1, 2+1, 3+2, 5+3, 10+5; Bullet, Blitz and Rapid are places 0,
  2 and 4; the result is the average of the two picks' places. That one line gives every case Eric listed: the same pick
  is that control, neighbors give the one between, Bullet against Rapid gives Blitz; one pick counts alone; none is
  `defaultControl` (Blitz 3+2). The unit test walks every pair and every missing pick.
- **The in-betweens' names:** "Bullet 2+1" and "Blitz 5+3", by the usual speed line (estimated length = minutes × 60 +
  40 × increment: 2+1 is 160 s, under Blitz's 180; 5+3 is 420 s, under Rapid's 480). Short, and no new word to learn.
- **The overlay's text:** "Timer: " + the control: "Timer: Bullet 1+1", "Timer: Bullet 2+1", "Timer: Blitz 3+2",
  "Timer: Blitz 5+3", "Timer: Rapid 10+5". 1.5 s over the board, a line, not a banner.
- **The zones:** the other pawn votes' zones, unchanged (`voteZoneBox`): files b-c, d-e and f-g on ranks 4 and 5, the
  board's empty middle, labeled at each zone's far edge "Bullet 1+1", "Blitz 3+2", "Rapid 10+5" (the speed and its
  control, on one line, sized in eighths of the board so they never overlap at 360 px). Bullet is on the left and Rapid
  on the right from both sides (`voteZoneFor` mirrors Black's view), as Eric asked. (Squads' clock vote is
  slowest-left today, Eric Oct 13; the home screen helper moves the other votes to Bullet-left.)
- **The pawns' spots** (the coordinator's call): each player's pawn starts off the grid on their own side, on the line
  between their second and third ranks, centered between the d- and e-files (`duelPawnHome`), outside every zone, so
  it plainly doesn't belong there. The other player's pawn moves live as they pick. Checked at 360 px: both pawns and
  the three labels are clear of each other.
- **Picking:** drag the pawn into a zone (it stays where it's dropped), or tap it and then tap a zone ("Pick Bullet
  1+1" buttons for screen readers). It can change until the 5 s are up. Picks go to the server as they're made; the
  server decides the control.
- **The first-time hint** (the coordinator's call): "Drag your pawn into the speed you want", once per device
  (`brc.voteHintSeen`), until the pawn is dropped or the vote ends. Built as a shared piece (`VoteHint`,
  `useVoteHint`, `voteHintSeen`, `markVoteHintSeen`) for the other pawn votes to use later. Without storage (some
  private modes): once per page load.

## The game's rules

- **Clocks start after Black's first move** (as on the big chess sites); before that each first move has 20 s
  (`firstMoveSeconds`; White's from the start, Black's from White's move) or the game is aborted. Aborting is free: no
  rank change, nothing recorded, no penalty. Before both first moves the match menu's exit is "Leave" (an abort);
  after, "Resign". Repeated dodging isn't penalized at launch: with few players a penalty would mostly hit people
  whose phone rang. Revisit if aborts pile up.
- **Flagging:** a loss, but a draw when the side still on time has a lone king, or a king and a single knight or
  bishop (`canMate`). That is a simplification of FIDE's "no sequence of legal moves could mate" (which would hand a
  king and knight a win against a king and pawns, by a helpmate), chosen because it is easy to explain in one line and
  to check.
- **Automatic draws:** stalemate, threefold repetition (positions counted by the first four FEN fields; chess.js only
  writes an en passant square when the capture is legal, so positions compare correctly), the 50-move rule at 100
  half-moves (a mate on that move still wins), not enough material. No claiming: they end the game when they happen.
- **Draw offers:** one at a time; an offer lapses when the other player moves; after a decline or a lapse its maker can
  offer again after 3 more moves of their own (`drawOfferEveryMoves`), so offers can't be spammed.
- **Disconnects:** the clock keeps running (Eric's rule); back in, they're in the same game (a reload, or another
  device signed in to that account). No "claim victory" button: the flag ends a game whose player never comes back, and
  the first-move abort covers a player gone from the start.
- **No lag compensation:** the server times each move as it arrives. Simple and fair to both; the cost is a few
  hundredths a move on a slow connection. The app shows the clocks from the server's numbers and its time offset.
- **Moves carry their ply**, so a move resent after a reconnect is never played twice, and a move for a ply the game
  isn't at is refused (the app's board goes back to the game).

## Ranks

- **The hidden rating: Glicko-1**, each game its own rating period. Why: it is the simplest rating that moves a new
  player fast and a settled one slowly on its own (the deviation, RD), which placements need, without the K-factor
  schedules Elo would need; Glicko-2's volatility adds an iterative solve that buys little at our numbers. Start: the
  account's `users.rating` from other modes (clamped 800-2400) or 1500, RD 350, down to 60, back up 18 a day idle
  (√(RD² + 18² × days)).
- **The ladder is one integer** per player: division index × 100 + rank points (Iron 4 0 = 0, Diamond 1 99 = 2799,
  Master from 2800). One number makes promotion, demotion and the floor plain arithmetic, and the label is worked out
  from it (`rankView`: "Gold 2 · 45 RP", "Master · 120 RP", "Placement 2/5").
- **Rank points a game:** 2 × 20 × (the result − the expected result from the two hidden ratings), so ±20 between
  equals, more for beating someone stronger and less for beating someone weaker (Elo's rule, on the ladder); plus
  0.05 × (hidden rating − the ladder's rating) within ±10 (a draw gets half the adjustment), which pulls the ladder to
  the hidden rating. The ladder's rating maps the ladder onto the rating scale: 800 at Iron 4 0, +1600/2800 a point,
  so Master's start is 2400. A win never takes points and a loss never gives them.
- **Why the expected result is in it** (changed from the design summary's flat ±20 after the simulation): with flat
  ±20, the points don't care whom you beat, so a player who keeps beating weaker opponents climbs past their hidden
  rating until the −10 adjustment balances their win rate, and past 75% wins nothing balances it. Two accounts trading
  wins in a quiet queue reached Champion 618 RP in 148 games (the range then kept them apart). With the expected result,
  those wins pay almost nothing once the ladder is level with the hidden rating: the same two accounts stop at
  Emerald 3, where the winner's hidden rating (2035) is. Between equals nothing changes: still about ±20, Eric's
  number.
- **Promotion and demotion:** crossing 100 is the next division with the extra carried, below 0 the one before (Gold 4
  −5 is Silver 1 95). No promotion series, no demotion shields. The floor is Iron 4 0.
- **Master and up** need nothing special: Master from 2800 (no divisions), Grandmaster from 200 RP above Master's
  start, Champion from 500 (`apex`). No top-N leaderboard rule: there aren't enough players for one to mean anything.
  Falling below Master's 0 is Diamond 1 again.
- **Placements:** the first 5 rated games show "Placement N/5" (aborted games don't count); the fifth sets the ladder
  from the hidden rating (`placeLadder`), at most Diamond 4 0, so Master and up are earned by play.
- **A player in fair-play review** keeps their rank as it is: their finished games don't move it (their opponent's does
  move as usual).
- **Measured** (the ranked lane's rule: `packages/sim/scripts/ranked-1v1-sim.ts`, `reports/ranked-1v1.md`; 1,500
  synthetic players with a known strength, through core's own functions):
  - the hidden rating is within 131 of the true strength (median) after the 5 placements, 81 after 20 games, 71 after
    40; the rank's rating within 123, 88 and 65; 67% are in their true tier after 40 games;
  - the ranks keep the players' order (rank correlation 0.98) and most players are in the middle (Bronze to Emerald
    80%; Master and up 1.7%). Pairing by the closest rating stretches the scale (the strong rated above their true
    number, the weak below; results between near-equals say little about the scale), so the tails hold more than the
    true strengths would. Ranks are relative, so it costs little; the tiers' cut points are settings
    (`baseRating`, `ratingPerPoint`) if the shares need moving once real players are in;
  - **sandbagging doesn't pay:** losing all 5 placements on purpose leaves a player 10 divisions below their honest
    twin when placed, still 4 below after 40 games, never ahead;
  - **trading wins** is bounded by the hidden rating (above); it remains a fair-play matter (reports), as in every
    ladder;
  - **leaving** gains nothing: resigning is a loss, and an abort (only before both first moves) records nothing; bots
    are unranked.
- **Not Weng–Lin / OpenSkill** (the ranked lane's suggestion): its strength is teams and free-for-alls; for one player
  against one it reduces to what Glicko-1 already does, with more to explain.
- **Only ranked games against people count.** No `results` rows for 1 v 1, so Crowd's stats and `users.rating` are
  unchanged; 1 v 1 has its own rank (50 v 50 and Squads join the tiers later, each with its own).

## Pairing

- By the hidden rating: the longest wait first, the closest rating within range. The range is `startRange` (100) +
  `perSecond` (10) × seconds waited + `perRd` (1) × the larger of the two deviations, at most `maxRange` (1000).
- **The deviation term was added after the first e2e run:** two new players who each played one placement game had
  drifted about 320 apart (the winner up 160, the loser down 160), and Play again kept them apart for 22 s with only
  the two of them online. Their ratings are still guesses (RD near 290), and a gap inside that says nothing, so it
  shouldn't keep them apart: with the term they meet at once; settled players (RD 60) start at 160. Measured: with 4
  players queueing at once the median wait is 0 s and the 90th percentile 28 s (gap 167, 90th percentile 631); with 16,
  5 s (gap 55); with 40, 0 s (gap 24).
- One ticket per account (a second tab is the same ticket); an account never meets itself.
- **Few players:** after 45 s, "Few players online right now" with Keep waiting and Play a bot (unranked). The bot game
  runs on the device by the same rules: Maia at the player's hidden rating (600-2500), Stockfish at that strength while
  Maia loads or outside that range. Its strength isn't calibrated against our hidden ratings yet; it is unranked, so a
  miss costs nothing.

## Coins

Eric's rule is "the same as other online matches"; the coordinator's: line it up with what Squads pays per minute, so
1 v 1 isn't the best coin farm. Squads pays a placement reward to each person on a squad: 100 (1st), 60 (2nd), 40
(3rd-4th), 20 (5th-8th). Per minute of a person's time at the Blitz clock (the median lobby takes 33.5 minutes,
`docs/areas/squads.md`; a squad out after round 1 has played about 15 minutes, after round 2 about 24):

| Squads finish | Coins | Minutes played | Coins a minute |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5th-8th (4 squads) | 20 | ~15 | 1.3 |
| 3rd-4th (2 squads) | 40 | ~24 | 1.7 |
| 2nd | 60 | ~33.5 | 1.8 |
| 1st | 100 | ~33.5 | 3.0 |
| Every squad together | 40 on average | ~21.9 on average | **~1.8** |

1 v 1 pays **per minute of the game** (from its start to its end) by result: **win 2.4, draw 1.6, loss 0.8** a minute
(an even record averages 1.6, a little under Squads' 1.8), **at least 1, at most 70** a game, and **nothing unless both
sides made 10 moves**; nothing for an aborted game or a bot game. Typical games:

| Control | Typical length | Win | Draw | Loss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bullet 1+1 | ~2.5 min | 6 | 4 | 2 |
| Bullet 2+1 | ~4 min | 10 | 6 | 3 |
| Blitz 3+2 | ~6 min | 14 | 10 | 5 |
| Blitz 5+3 | ~10 min | 24 | 16 | 8 |
| Rapid 10+5 | ~19 min | 46 | 30 | 15 |

- **Why per minute, not flat 20/10/5:** a flat amount would make Bullet pay three to six times what Rapid does per
  minute; per minute keeps every control at the same rate as Squads, whatever is voted.
- **No daily cap** (the design summary had 20 games a day): with pay by the minute, a cap of games limits nothing about
  the rate, and farming doesn't beat playing. Two accounts trading wins earn (2.4 + 0.8) / 2 = 1.6 a minute each, the
  honest rate, and must play 10 moves each in real time to earn anything; resigning at once pays nothing. The 70 cap
  stops a stalled Rapid game from paying more than a long real one. Without a cap there's no counter to store.
- Coins are headed for real-money pricing, so Eric will want to see these rates; they're all in `RANKED_1V1.coins`.

## Data, privacy and retention

- `ranked_1v1` (one row per player: hidden rating, RD, record, ladder) lives with the account, is in the download,
  goes with deletion; a guest's row moves to the account on sign-in only if the account has none (guests can't queue
  where sign-in is set up, but local and test servers allow them).
- `ranked_1v1_games` keeps each ranked game's moves and think times 30 days (`gamesKeepDays`), longer while a player in
  it is in review or banned (the evidence), then the 15-minute cron deletes it. Deleting an account turns that player
  into "deleted" in their opponents' games. The download has a player's games, the opponent by name, never fair play's
  check. The privacy draft has a "1 v 1 (ranked)" entry.
- The game object deletes everything it stored 10 minutes after the result (`resultsKeepMinutes`).

## Fair play

- At launch: reports from the profile (as everywhere; one opened from the game names it), and the case page's
  existing "Check the counted moves with the engine server" button now also checks the player's kept 1 v 1 games,
  reported ones first, after the book moves, every legal move as a candidate. It spends the same daily budget as the
  deep re-check (no new cap, no paid service), and its result is shown to the reviewer, never fed into the suspicion
  score (calibrating that for 1 v 1 needs data we don't have yet). Checking every game needs Eric's approval with a
  monthly estimate first, so it isn't built.

## The kill switch

`PAUSE_RANKED1V1` on #238's mechanism: the queue answers "1 v 1 is resting for a bit. Back soon." and the app shows it
without queueing; games already running finish.
