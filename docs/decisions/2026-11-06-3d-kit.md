# The 3D art kit (2026-11-06)

Eric, Nov 6: "how does an N64 or a Game Boy Advance build these in 3D space and they look totally fine? … the claw machine looks out of place … old school … pixelated." One kit for every room's 3D, generalizing the street town's boxes (the audit of Nov 6, §3); no new engine, no library.
- `town/kit/`: `prims.ts` (box, prism, pyramid/hip, wedge, n-gon cylinder, dome, quad), `bake.ts` (a pixel grid on every face at 37 texels a square, light as palette ramp steps, a 1-texel ink outline), `draw.ts` (through the Painter's `Ctx` and `proj`; nearest at 1.5x and up), models as data (`{ palette, textures, shapes, anchors }`).
- *(mine)* Light: tops and lit slopes the light step, fronts and left sides the base, a side turned right of the camera one step darker, roofs quantized from n·L (kept: the town's light vector). The left sides' 6% shade becomes the base; turning walls snap between two steps.
- *(mine)* A plain palette color's ramp is derived once: dark = the art's 30% shade (so right-hand walls are unchanged), light = 1.06x. A model may give its own ramps from its home's palette (the claw does).
- The street town's box path goes through the kit: roofs bake from palette keys (no multiply), walls in shade are the frame with its palette one step darker (no #0b0a12 overlay), the ink edge and rim are palette colors.
