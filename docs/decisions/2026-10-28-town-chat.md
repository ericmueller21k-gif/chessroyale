# The town square's chat log (Oct 28, 2026)

Phone and computer (Eric described both). Follows the town bots (`2026-10-27-town-bots.md`) and Eric's frame-rate rule
(`docs/rules/eric.md`, "The town square").

## Eric's words (Oct 28, trimmed)

"I mentioned adding a chat feature. We'll have to figure out later how to actually chat on mobile … but for now … the
animals and the bosses can put messages in here, like once in a while, just so we can see. … what other MMORPG style do …
a little timestamp of the message, the person's name, the message, and then they kind of fade, and then when nobody said
something for like a few sec … they fade, and then when somebody says something again, it like appears again in the top
right, like the most recent messages. … on mobile … show at most like 4 to 5 messages. On desktop … closer to like a dozen
and can be a bit bigger of the space."

Then, after he saw where it goes (relayed by the director the same day, paraphrased): the log goes in the **top left**, not
the top right (his outline on a phone: from the map's left edge to about two thirds of its width, from the map's top down
to about a fifth of its height, over the Colosseum's top; on a computer the same corner, wider and taller for about 12
lines); a **text field** at the box's foot to type in; the box can be **picked up and moved**; lines are **removed for good
after 60 seconds**, not just faded ("it keeps the DOM small"); and players will **report a message by tapping it**.

## What it is

A fading log in the map's top left corner, over the map and under the town's cards, in its own files: `town/chat.ts` (the
lines, the numbers, the rules; no DOM), `town/TownChat.tsx` (the screen), `town/town-chat.css`, one mount in `Town.tsx` and
one extra check where the edge chips are placed in `render.ts`. How it works and where everything is:
`docs/areas/town-square.md`, "The chat log".

- **A line**: "[9:41] Name: message", the name bold, on its own soft dark backing, newest at the bottom, at most two lines
  then "…". **At most 5 lines on a phone and 12 on a computer.**
- **Fading**: `fadeAfterMs` (8 s) after the last message the lines fade out over `fadeMs` (1 s); the next message brings the
  recent lines back. A line is gone for good `lifeMs` (60 s) after it was said, or when pushed past the cap.
- **Who talks for now**: every 20 to 45 s one of the 14 bosses with a home, or its kid, says a line from `CHATTER`: two lines a
  boss, one or two a kid, all under 60 characters, placeholders until real players chat (marked so in the table).
- **One way in**: `say(name, text, kind)` (kind: boss, kid, player, system). Nothing is sent to the server or read from it.
- **Your own line**: a text field at the box's foot, **only where the test switches are allowed** (admins, the test server,
  dev); `sendTownChat(text)` echoes it on this device and returns ok or the reason it refused, so the server lane swaps its
  inside. Players see the log with the chatter only.
- **Moving it**: press and hold on the box (400 ms), drag, drop; remembered on this device.
- **Player lines take taps** (`onTownChatLineTap(line, anchor)`, which does nothing yet: the server lane's Profile / Mute /
  Report menu).

## Calls I made

1. **The time** is the device's clock when it saw the message, hours and minutes, 12-hour, no am/pm (the brief's call): "9:41",
   "12:05" after midnight. Brackets in the line, the name bold.
2. **Name colors** by kind, so bosses and kids tell apart at a glance: gold for a boss, mint for a kid, light blue for a
   player, italic and plain for the town's own lines. Light enough to read on the dark backing over any part of the map.
3. **The box's size** (phone): the width Eric outlined (about two thirds, `66%` of the map less the margin, at most 300 px), and a
   height of 23% of the map (a bit over his fifth, because five two-line messages and the field need it; at 390 × 844 it is
   182 px of 792). On a computer 500 × 340 with 14 px type, so most lines fit on one row. "Computer" is `styles.css`' own
   query (`min-width: 900px` and `min-height: 600px`), kept as `CHAT.computerQuery`.
4. **Lines sit at the box's foot**, just above the field's strip, the oldest slipping off the top under a soft mask when two-line
   messages don't all fit (the DOM holds at most 5 or 12; a phone shows four or five depending on how many wrap). The box
   itself never changes size or place whatever it holds (Eric's rule), and **the field's strip is kept for everyone**, so a
   player who gets the field late (the account loads after the town opens), or an admin who signs in, moves nothing.
5. **Fade numbers**: 8 s wait, 1 s out, 0.2 s in (`CHAT`), the brief's "about". Under reduced motion, no transition.
6. **The chatter**: anyone but the one who spoke last, a line of theirs at random, the first one 20 to 45 s after the town opens.
   The kids' names are the sidekicks brief's and stay in `CHATTER` (keyed by home) until the sidekicks are in `BOTS`; a unit test
   keeps every boss with a home covered, every line under 60 characters, and the Hidden Boss out. Hollow's kid (Glim) is only
   eerie ("The dark taught me a good opening.", "Shh. The dark is thinking."), as Hollow says of it. The bronze statue's
   subject is never named; no line mentions real people; no "Soviet" or "Russian" anywhere (a unit test checks).
7. **The field**: `maxlength` 80 and a count ("7 left") only for the last 10, shown inside the field's right end so nothing
   moves; the keyboard's own Send / Enter sends (`enterkeyhint="send"`; a form with one field, no button); one message per 15
   s, the wait counting down in the field as its placeholder ("Wait 12 s") with the field greyed, so there is nothing to
   refuse; after a send the field lets go (the keyboard closes) and empties. 16 px type, so iPhone Safari doesn't zoom in.
   Your line is said as `playerName()`, kind "player".
8. **The keyboard** must not resize or move the town. While the field has focus the town's screen keeps the height it had (a
   browser that shrinks the page for the keyboard would shrink it too) and slides with `visualViewport` if the browser pans it
   to show the field (`pinTown`); a press on the map lets the field go. Headless can't open a soft keyboard: the e2e stands in
   for the pan by setting the visual viewport's offset. **Eric's real-phone check is still to do** (iPhone Safari and Android
   Chrome, the field near the top, then the box moved low on the map).
9. **Moving the box**: the town's map listens for the press (the box takes no taps, so the press goes to the map first). A
   press that stays within 8 px for 400 ms picks the box up (an outline shows, the lines show, the map's press is called off
   with a `pointercancel`, so nothing walks); a drag that starts without the hold, and a tap, are the map's as before. Dropped, it's
   saved (`brc.townChat`, whole pixels, in a try/catch) and clamped inside the map, 8 px from the edges and off the buttons
   (−, +, "Back to me": it ends beside or above them, whichever is nearer). A faded box with no field can't be seen, so a press
   there is the map's however long. The press-and-hold also works from a player's line.
10. **The edge chips**: the box's rect (`CHAT_BOX`, one object `TownChat` writes and `chipBlocked` reads) is reserved for them
    always, shown or faded, wherever the box is put. The cost: the chips along the top and left edges slide away from the
    corner (they already slide for name tags and the buttons), and with the box dragged to the middle of the map the
    chips that would lie under it slide too.
11. **Player lines take taps** (`pointer-events` on that line only, and only while the log shows; a faded one lets taps through);
    the boss's and kid's lines and the log's empty space pass them to the map. A tap is a quick press and release without
    moving; `window.__townTapped` lists them for the e2e.
12. **Test hooks** (test switches on): `__townSay()` makes someone talk now, `__townSay(name, text, kind)` says that line,
    `__townChatter(false)` stops the chatter of its own while a test looks.

13. **A short screen**: for the people who get the field (the test switches on), its strip takes taps, so on a screen as short as
    the e2e's phone (390 × 664) it lies over the Colosseum's gate and the marble statue. Players have no field, and on a
    390 × 844 phone it sits above both; move the box (press and hold) if it's in the way.
14. **What's not in the frame loop**: the log is DOM drawn when a message comes (a few a minute); the fade is a CSS opacity
    transition; the chatter and the fade are `setTimeout`s; the only thing the renderer does with it is one more rectangle
    test per chip slot per frame in `chipBlocked` (no allocation).

## Frame rate (Eric's rule)

Measured headless (software rendering) by the method of `2026-10-26-town-square-slice-2.md`: a phone 390 × 844 and a computer
1440 × 900, 5 s walking (a tap ahead every 1.2 s), builds served statically, the runs taken in turn. The machine was busy
with other helpers, so each case was run twice over (main twice, the branch twice) as a noise control and the five runs of
each pooled (ten runs a case; medians). Main thread = the page's main thread per frame (Chrome's `TaskDuration`).

| Case | Phone | Computer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| main, walking | 50.5 fps (19.7 ms) | 38.2 fps (26.3 ms) |
| this branch, walking, **three messages arriving during the run** (at 1, 2.5 and 4 s) | 50.9 fps (19.6 ms) | 38.4 fps (26.2 ms) |
| main, standing still | 53.5 fps (18.6 ms) | 35.5 fps (28.2 ms) |
| this branch, standing still, **the text field focused (the keyboard up, as far as headless goes) and typing**, messages arriving | 52.9 fps (18.9 ms) | 34.6 fps (29.0 ms) |

The two runs of the same build differ by about 1 fps on a phone and 1 to 1.5 on a computer (main 50.8 and 50.2, 37.7 and
38.7; the branch 49.7 and 50.9, 38.9 and 37.6), more than the difference between main and the branch: no cost to speak of. The
town's start (the first frame drawn) is about 2.7 s on both builds, phone and computer (medians 2.67 to 2.73 s; a run in
ten reaches 3.3 s on either). As built, the chat has nothing in the frame loop; no
corner was cut. A real phone's soft keyboard can't be opened headless, so "keyboard up" here is the field focused and typed
into; Eric's real-phone check covers the rest.

## Test checks changed

No assertion of any existing test was edited, loosened or removed, and none guards a rule in `docs/rules/eric.md` that this
touches (the town's frame rate is measured, not a test; `e2e/town.spec.ts`'s check that nothing of Maia's is fetched in the
town is untouched). One harness line was added to `e2e/town.spec.ts`: a `beforeEach` that makes the chat's text field let taps
through (`.tc-form{pointer-events:none}`), because with the test switches on (as in every e2e) the field lies over a corner
of the map, and on the phone project's short screen (390 × 664) that corner holds the Colosseum's gate and the marble
statue, which "the Colosseum" and "the marble statue" tap. `e2e/town-chat.spec.ts` tests the field itself. New:
`packages/app/test/town-chat.test.ts` and `e2e/town-chat.spec.ts` (added to the town square's specs in `e2e/areas.json`).
