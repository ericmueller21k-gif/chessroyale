# The town square: bots you can play (Oct 27, 2026)

Phone and computer (Eric named neither). Follows the park's live tables (`2026-10-27-park-tables-live.md`) and the lights.

## Eric's words (Oct 27, trimmed)

"a few bots in each … world that walked around with different skill level and if you tapped on the bot it would show …
their level or their ELO … give them a hat or like one to two pieces of clothing … one to two of them sitting at a table and
then if you go to the other side you can challenge them or if you tap them you can invite them to a game and then you both
run over to the chessboard … load the Maya at whatever rank [on] the person's phone who does the challenge … the rankings
would be tied to the actual bot … put … in parentheses like bot after their name and their names could just be like a little

And his frame-rate rule, added to `docs/rules/eric.md` ("The town square"): "we don't want any frame drops from a clever
programming solution … we can always cut corners on this … if it keeps our FPS intact."

## What it is

- **Six bots, one table** (`BOTS` in `town/world.ts`: a name, a rating, a look; nowhere else): Pin Pete 800, Rook Rita
  1000, Fork Frank 1200, Tempo Tess 1400, Bishop Bo 1700, Gambit Gus 2000 (the brief's suggestion: 8 to 10 characters,
  spread from 800 to 2000, steps of 200 to 300). Shown everywhere as "<name> (bot)": their name tags in the town, their
  cards, the table's Play and the game's strip. The old names (Berlin Wally, Castle Cass, Gambit Gwen: 11 to 12
  characters) are gone; Fork Frank, Tempo Tess and Pin Pete stay.
- **Their clothes**: one or two crate items each, from the items your own pawn can wear, clean (no blemish, never shiny):
  Pin Pete a red beanie; Rook Rita a cobalt party hat and a yellow scarf; Fork Frank a red bandana and a fake mustache;
  Tempo Tess a sage wizard hat; Bishop Bo a midnight fedora and dark shades; Gambit Gus a yellow little crown (the
  strongest wears the crown). Each bot's dressed pawn is drawn once into a canvas as the page opens (`dressedPawn`, as
  yours), never per frame. Slice 5's "bots stay plain" is replaced by Eric's ask. A bot's `look` stays one field (side,
  tint, items), so a sidekick sprite can replace the pawn later without touching the flow.
- **A tap on a bot**: you walk beside it (as before) and its card opens over its name tag (the town's card, placed like a
  boss's): "Tempo Tess (bot)", "Rating 1400", **Play**. The bot stops at the square it's stepping onto and stays while the
  card is open; a tap elsewhere or Escape closes it.
- **Play**: you both walk to the nearest table with both stools free (your steps to your stool plus its steps to its
  own), you to the stool nearer you, the bot to the other. Once both sit, the game opens. Any other walk of yours on the
  way calls it off (the bot carries on to its stool and waits there). **No table free**: Play greyed and "Tables are full"
  under it; the line's room is always kept (blank when there's a table), so the card never changes size.
- **A bot sitting alone at a table**: a tap on the table walks you to its free stool (as before); its card's first button
  is **"Play Tempo Tess (bot) · 1400"**, Casual and Ranked greyed under it and "Coming soon" as before. The bot stays while
  the card is open. The game opens once you sit (straight away if you're already there).
- **The game** is the 1 v 1 bot game (`BotDuel`, `screens/Duel.tsx`) with a `town` option, on this device: the bot's name
  and look on its strip, its rating on its pill, no versus moment. First, **Boss alone's loading step** over the board
  ("Loading the bot…", the same bar, notes and Leave: `MaiaGate`, this device's Stockfish paused, Maia loaded alone, at most
  `BOSS_BRAIN.loadGateMs`; the same `maiaAllowed` rule). Then the bot game's vote and clock. Maia plays at the bot's
  rating, its own draw (`BOSS_BRAIN` temperature and top-p); until it's ready, or where it can't run, Stockfish at that
  rating (`playAtElo`), the bot game's own fallback. Colors at random. **Casual**: "Casual game", no rank change, no coins,
  nothing sent to the server (the bot game never sent anything). The gear: Leave before the first moves, Resign after.
- **After the game**: its result has one button, **"Back to town"**. The town opens with you on your stool at that table,
  the bot across, no card open; after `botLeaveAfterGameMs` (4 s) the bot gets up and wanders off (unless you've opened
  the table's card, which holds it: tapping the table then offers the same game again). **The back button** from the game
  and **a reload** land there too: the game's address is `/town?at=<table>.<stool>.<bot>` (one step of its own in the
  history); the town reads the seat and takes it out of the address.
- **The tables while you play**: you aren't in the town; when you come back the board shows still pieces, as for anyone
  sitting with you.

## Calls

- **The game is the bot game, not a new screen.** It's the normal plain-chess game screen we have (Ranked 1 v 1's), with
  its gear, board, strips, result and the bot that already plays Maia at a rating with a Stockfish fallback. A `town`
  option adds the name, the rating, the loading step and the way back: about 80 lines, no second path.
- **The clock: kept.** The brief: "no clock unless the existing solo path needs one". The bot game's rules (shared with the
  server's ranked game, `packages/chess/src/duel/rules.ts`) need a control from its vote; a game without one would mean
  changing the shared rules or a second game. So it's the bot game's: the 5-second vote (Bullet 1+1, Blitz 3+2, Rapid
  10+5; the bot picks at random) and its clock, the 20 s for each first move. The park's tables already show a clock over
  a game, so a clock fits.
- **The loading step before the vote**, so its time never comes off anyone's clock, and the bot's first move (if it has
  White) is Maia's when Maia loads in time.
- **Stockfish's floor**: `playAtElo` starts at 1320 (`ENGINE_MIN_ELO`), so where Maia can't run, Pin Pete, Rook Rita and
  Fork Frank play at 1320. The bot game's own fallback, kept as it is (the brief: reuse, don't build a second).
- **The pill is the number** ("1400"), not "Rating 1400": with "Tempo Tess (bot)" the longer pill cut the name on an
  iPhone 13 ("Tempo Tess …"). With the number every bot's whole name fits at 320, 360 and 390 px (measured). The card
  says "Rating 1400" in full.
- **No prefetch on Play.** Allowed by the brief, but the walk to the table is a few seconds; the loading step fetches the
  files anyway (saved after the first time). Simplest, and nothing of Maia's touches the town (the e2e checks it).
- **Back to town is one button** (no "Play again"): the table's card offers the same bot again while it's there.
- **"Casual game"** on the result (the table's word), rather than the bot game's "Unranked game".
- **The header stays "1 v 1 · Classic chess"**: it is a one-on-one game of classic chess.
- **Held bots stop on the next square** (`holdBot`): a bot walking when you tap it stops where you're headed to stand
  beside it.

## Frame rates (Eric's rule)

Measured as the slice 2 decision says: headless Chromium, the default view (`/town?sky=day`, zoomed out), warmed 4 s, then
5 s walking (a tap ahead every 1.25 s, left and right in turn); phone 390 × 844 at 3×, computer 1440 × 900; main and this
branch built and measured in turn (main, branch, main, branch…), frames a second, medians of four runs. A busy machine
(other agents running), so three rounds:

| | Phone main | Phone bots | Computer main | Computer bots |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Round 1 | 53.6 | 53.2 | 42.4 | 40.3 |
| Round 2 | 53.3 | 53.8 | 41.7 | 41.8 |
| Round 3 | 52.8 | 51.2 | 39.9 | 42.4 |
| All 12 runs | 53.3 | 53.2 | 41.6 | 41.6 |

(Round 3's first pair was hit by the machine: 28.7 / 25.6 on the phone and 6.1 on main's computer; the medians keep
them.) Round 3 also read the main thread's busy time a frame (Chrome's `TaskDuration`): phone 18.6 ms on main, 19.3 ms here;
computer 25.1 ms and 23.6 ms. The differences go both ways from round to round, within the noise between runs of the same
build (±3 fps): no cost to measure. What's new on the hot path: one map lookup a bot a frame (its dressed pawn, drawn as
yours is: one image, made once); while a bot's card is open, a check over the ten tables' stools (no search, nothing
allocated); while you and a bot walk to a table, whether both sit. The walk to the table (a search) runs once on Play.
Maia never loads in the town: only when the game has opened, which closes the town.
