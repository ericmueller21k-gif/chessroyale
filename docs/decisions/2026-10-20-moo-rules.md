# Moo & Moon in the rules: Moonbeam and the Full Moon (Oct 20, 2026)

A new raid boss (Eric, Oct 20): **Moo & Moon** (id `moo`), a sweet cow who can't talk and his friend, a chatty crescent
Moon. This builds his rules, the server side, the raid sim, the tests and the docs. His art and animations are on
`claude/moo-sprite` (docs/decisions/2026-10-20-moo-animations.md there); the wiring (kit, lines, sounds, tile, the beam
and its glow on the board, his powered-up look) is the next lane's. Until then he stays **not playable** (`kit: null`;
the test links `?boss=moo&wip=1`, with `&power=fullmoon` for his ultimate or `&power=moonbeam` for a beam after every
move). How it works now: [docs/areas/bosses.md](../areas/bosses.md) (his row); the tuning:
[reports/moo-sim.md](../../reports/moo-sim.md).

## What Eric decided

- **His opening:** the same start other bosses get (the game so far).
- **Moonbeam (passive):** every 3 of his moves, at the end of his turn, a beam of moonlight lands on one of the crowd's
  pieces; that piece can't move on the crowd's next turn (one turn; then it's free). "You can see which piece you can't
  move and possibly then capture or defend something." The target: the crowd's most useful piece. Never the king;
  always leave the crowd a legal move; a stunned piece still attacks and defends.
- **Full Moon (ultimate):** on the rage meter; at the start of his turn, after the usual warning, he transforms and plays
  his move (it doesn't take his turn). **Max Stockfish for 5 of his moves** ("we may even need to make it like 7").
  On the first of those turns, at the end of it, a **flurry of 5 moonbeams** on 5 different crowd pieces, each stunned
  for the crowd's next turn. After the 5 moves he's **dazed** and back to normal strength.

## The calls made here, and why

1. **The beam's engine work rides on his move's request.** "The crowd's engine-best move from the position after his
   move" needs an engine after his move and before the crowd's turn. Rather than a new step (a host round trip of its
   own and a new runner step for the kit's driver), the boss request carries `beam: 8` when a beam lands after this
   move, and the host answers its move with the crowd's top 8 moves after it (`beamLines`: one MultiPV search at
   `moonbeamNodes`, 50,000 nodes, about a tenth to a third of a second on a phone, after his own search). The server
   checks every line (legal there, a crowd piece's, not the king's) and picks (`chooseBeams`); solo and the sims do the
   same on their own engine. An older app ignores `beam` and answers no lines, and a host that times out gets a random
   move from the server: then the beams go by value. The server never searches.
2. **"Most useful":** the piece that moves in the crowd's best line (the line with the highest score, not the first
   slot: the lines are sorted by score, ties by move); then the pieces of the next lines in turn; then the crowd's other
   pieces with a move, by value (queen 9, rook 5, bishop and knight 3, pawn 1), then by how many moves each has, ties
   from the seed (`beamOrder`). The flurry takes the same order. Only pieces with a legal move are ever beamed: a stun
   on a piece that can't move anyway (in check, only the pieces that answer it can) would do nothing.
3. **Always a move left:** each beam is kept only if the crowd still has a legal move with every stunned piece held; if
   the most useful piece would leave none, the next is tried; if none works, no beam that turn (a check only one piece
   can answer). **"The count carries on"**: the beams come after his 3rd, 6th, 9th… move of the battle whatever
   happened before, so a turn without a beam doesn't push the next one back.
4. **One crowd turn, exactly:** the stun is set for crowd turn `crowdMoves + 1` and is gone as the crowd's move lands
   (`afterCrowdMove`), so the glow is off while he thinks. A God King's Last Stand takes the crowd's move back: the same
   turn is picked again and the stun stays for it.
5. **Reused from Ginger's freeze:** how the crowd's moves leave stunned pieces out (`crowdFilter`, so `crowdAllowed`):
   the round's `allowed` goes to everyone, to the score jobs online, so the judge scores only allowed moves (its best
   is the best allowed: `judgeTop`, `judgeCandidates`, `allowedSearch`), the bots pick only from them, the God King
   plays the judge's best, and nobody is scored against a move a stunned piece can't make; and `powerTurn` (a stunned
   turn doesn't count for fair play). The dispatcher's lift (no move left: the limit lifts) is kept but never needed.
   **Not reused:** the `iced` field (the app draws ice for it): his squares go in their own `stunned`; and the freeze's
   start-of-turn step: his beam lands at the end of his turn (`afterBossMove`, as Smudgy's swap), its moment as the
   crowd's turn begins (`everyTurn`, as Smudgy's). The dock's words for a refused move: a new line next to "That piece
   is frozen.": **"Moonlight holds that piece."** (`REFUSED.stunned`, `refused.ts`).
6. **The Full Moon comes as the crowd's move lands** (`afterCrowdMove`, the way Smudgy's `rookTaken` plays while he
   thinks), not as a new step: no host round trip, no new runner step. Its moment, `fullMoon`, is among the events of
   his thinking, and his thinking lasts at least its length (`fullMoonMs`, solo and online, as Alfil's early landing).
   The warning comes as a crowd turn begins, as for every meter (Smudgy's and Hollow's way: `ultimateOnHisTurn`, the
   admins' trigger kept until then). Not if the crowd's move ended the battle.
7. **"Max" strength is the runner's existing "best"** (`BossRules.bestReply`, Wrangler's reply to the showdown's duel):
   **Stockfish at full strength (no `UCI_LimitStrength`, no Elo cap), one line at the boss's own per-move budget,
   `bossNodes` = 250,000 nodes** (the sims: 120,000), on the host's device; never Maia (no brain is sent with it), no
   slips, no blunder guard. That is the strongest the boss brain is ever allowed: every usual move of his already
   searches 250,000 nodes for its top 8 lines (`engineNodes`), and again at `bossNodes` for a limited-strength pick, so
   one full-strength line at `bossNodes` is no more engine work than his usual move and fits his move time on any host. Boss alone's difficulty doesn't change it. More nodes would be stronger but would
   cost a slow phone more than his usual move, so they stay as they are.
8. **The God King's strike still staggers** a Full Moon move (the strike's step back beats the moon), and that move
   counts as one of the 5.
9. **The flurry** is on the first Full Moon turn, at its end (the director's call, told to Eric), in place of that turn's
   Moonbeam if both fall due; the Moonbeam's count goes on through the Full Moon (that turn counts as one of his moves).
   Fewer than 5 only when fewer pieces have a move or more would leave none. Its moment, `flurry`, lists the squares in
   the order they land (the app staggers them 120 ms apart, `MOO_BEATS.flurry.stagger`).
10. **Dazed** is a moment only (the director's proposal: "the dazed animation and a line, back to normal strength, no
    weaker move"), as the crowd's turn after his 5th move at full strength begins.
11. **The meter while it lasts:** held empty from the transformation until his last move at full strength
    (`ultimateRunning`, as Ginger's blizzard and every lasting ultimate), and the God King's strike adds nothing to it;
    then it fills again from empty. No cap a battle (as most bosses).
12. **Test switches:** `?power=fullmoon` warns as the second turn begins (the usual test switch: the Full Moon at the start
    of his turn after it); `?power=moonbeam` brings a beam after **every** move of his (as Wrangler's drag and Alfil's
    Tusks come sooner on theirs), so a test sees one at once.
13. **Protocol:** optional fields only. `bossRequest.beam` (how many lines), `bossMove.beam` (the lines), and in
    `NetBossPowers` `stunned` (the squares held now, in the order the beams landed; `[]` once the crowd's move lands)
    and `fullMoon` (`{ left }`: his moves still at full strength, this one included while he thinks; null: none). Old
    apps ignore them.
14. **Moments** (`MOO_FX`, from his animations, the longest of the animation and its last beam's fall (220 ms) and hit
    (240 ms), so the server waits for the board's beams too): `moonbeam` 1040 ms (`square`), `flurry` 1430 ms
    (`squares`, in order), `fullMoon` 1340 ms (while he thinks), `dazed` 1250 ms.
15. **The app side here is only what compiles and plays:** an empty `components/Moo.tsx`, his power words, the refusal
    line, solo's thinking waiting for the Full Moon and the host's `beam` answer. Everything drawn is the wiring lane's.

## The numbers

From the raid sim ([reports/moo-sim.md](../../reports/moo-sim.md); 26 battles a run, the bots at tier 1600):

- **He lands outside the pack, far too strong:** at offset −250 and `rageSpeed` 1 the crowd scores **0.02 ± 0.02** in
  **33** crowd moves (25 boss wins, 1 draw). With the meter at half speed (`rageSpeed` 0.5: 0.54 Full Moons a battle
  instead of 1.42, 12 battles with none) it scores **0.00**: the 12 battles without a Full Moon were his too, so the
  Moonbeam as specified (the crowd's engine-best piece every 3rd move) wins on its own against this crowd.
- **Each Full Moon costs the crowd about 6.5 pawns** of material (mean and median, from its first move to the move after
  his 5th at full strength); at the first one the crowd was still even or ahead in 15 battles of 25. 5 moves are more
  than enough; 7 would be stronger still. The flurry stunned all 5 pieces in 29 of 34.
- **Beams:** 8.8 a battle (3 to 17), about one crowd move in four.
- **Kept: offset −250** (he plays at 1350, by Stockfish's floor of 1320: a lower offset has no lever left at this tier,
  as Alfil's sim found) **and `rageSpeed.moo` 1** (an ultimate or two a battle, like the pack; slower changes nothing).
  `moonbeamEvery` 3, `fullMoonMoves` 5 and `flurryBeams` 5 are Eric's, so they stay; what would bring him into the
  pack changes one of them, so it's **Eric's call**: the Moonbeam every 4 or 5 of his moves, or on a less useful piece
  (Ginger's way: one of the crowd's three most valuable mobile pieces), and the Full Moon 3 moves or without the
  flurry. Each is one setting and a 13-minute rerun. Not playable until his kit is wired, so nobody meets him meanwhile.
- **`moonbeamLines` 8** (the judge's and the bots' top-8, enough lines for five different pieces as a rule; the flurry
  still found 5 in 29 of 34) and **`moonbeamNodes` 50,000** (a quick look, after his own move, on the host).
