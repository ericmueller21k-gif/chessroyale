Main went red after #718 (65902dc3) with two town tests. Both causes were timing between the test and the game; the game itself was right both times.

**Cause and fix**
- **`[phone] town-chat.spec.ts:1054`: "the line replaced" failed on both tries.** The CI trace shows the second refused try was actually *sent* ("Player: too soon …" is in the log). On that runner each Playwright step took about 0.4 s. The style checks between the first and second try (about 14 s, plus the 2.2 s wait) pushed the second Enter past the 15 s wait, so the message went through and no new gray line came. The game is right: a second try inside the wait puts a new line (a new `data-id`) in the old one's place, with N as the wait left at that moment.
  - The test now makes both tries straight after the send, before any other check.
  - It checks the line was replaced: a different `data-id`, still one line.
  - It bounds each try's N by the wait left as that try came: the server's 15 s from its answer, which arrived between the press and your line showing.
  - Nothing depends on a second boundary, and the 2.2 s `waitForTimeout` is gone. The later "past the 15 s" wait now counts from when your line showed.
- **`[desktop] town.spec.ts:966`: "bot 9 is drawn" failed on the first try.** Bot 9 is Tempo Tess, called while Glim already stands beside you. `__townCall` sent her to the first free square beside you she could reach. Coming from straight behind you, that is the square behind your pawn, which hides her, so no tap point is found. The test hook now picks a free square beside you off the camera's line through you (your column when looking north or south, your row when looking east or west). If none is free it falls back to any square beside you.
  - Only the test hook changed (`spotNear` gets an optional offset filter). A player's own walk is untouched.
  - Not reproduced locally: every fresh town here puts the bots in the same places (Tess stops left of you every time), so the CI frame can't be recreated. The cause is read from the code and the retry's trace.
