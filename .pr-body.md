## Dash's Scramble Rush: solo counts only you, and his extra move always matches the verdict

Eric's Nov 8 playtest (solo): he failed the put-back and Dash's line said so, but Dash didn't take his extra move.

### The cause
The verdict (`rush.extra = "due"`, which drives the dock's "too few: He moves again" and Dash's `fail` line) and the move
itself were decided separately. After the verdict, Hollow's extra-move rules could still **skip** the move without a word:
- no quiet move within Hollow's 3-point cap (`extraMoveFrom` → null),
- the crowd in check after his move (`passTurn` can't pass a turn in check; about 1 in 16 of his turns in a real-engine probe),
- in solo, any engine error in `extraTurn` (`.catch(() => applyExtraMove(null))` → skip).
The screen kept saying he'd move again. In a solo raid the bots were also in the mean, so your own fail could be outvoted.

### What changed
- **Solo: only your own put-back counts** (`finishRush(places, only)`; solo passes you while you're in). No bots, no mean.
  Online the crowd's mean stays.
- **Once due, he never skips it** (`applyExtraMove` / `dashExtraMoveFrom`): a quiet move within the cap if there is one,
  else the quiet move closest to it (no forced mate). With no engine answer, a quiet move from the seed (`rushStandIn`).
  Online the host uses the same rule, and the server's stand-in covers a host that sends "".
- **The Rush waits out a check**: still warned, it comes on the next crowd turn that isn't in check.
- **No quiet move at all** (very rare): `"skipped"` is set in the verdict itself. The dock then says "too few", "No quiet move
  for him: your move.", and Dash's fail line doesn't play.
- **Dock line online** shows the crowd's verdict: "Crowd: 3.4 of 6: too few", plus "(On average; you: 5.)". Solo it's yours
  as before.
- **Move list numbers** come from each row's own position (`rowNumber`), so his extra move reads "14…" after his "13…"
  (by ply parity it read "14.").

### Player-visible
- Solo: fail the Rush and Dash always moves twice; the move list shows both of his moves in a row.
- Online: the dock's verdict names the crowd's average; the line and the move never disagree.
- FAQ: "(solo, yours alone; online with a crowd, on average) … the move list shows both of his moves".
