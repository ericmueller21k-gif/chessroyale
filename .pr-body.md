## What changed

The Hidden Boss's phase change is now his transformation by the book's light (Eric's mockup rounds 1 to 3b and the eyes, Nov 14; stills and GIFs on `stills/recluse-transform-book`). Boss lane. Decision: `docs/decisions/2026-11-14-recluse-transformation.md`; area page: `docs/areas/bosses.md`.

- **It carries on from his cutscene with no break.** The book at his feet opens. On the music's slam the light takes him and **his eyes go white**. On its build he is lifted off his knees, **his arms relaxed at his sides the whole time**. The light grows until it shoots up off the screen, radiates over the whole screen and **whites the screen out**.
- **The change happens unseen.** In the white he's set down in the suit and helmet, and the child and the lens go. The white dies back into the center and shows him.
- **His line with his face showing.** His visor flips up (his **eyes glowing blue**, a line of light down each cheek) for "Computation without creativity is nothing. Now I have both.", then flips back down; the line stays up through the crouch until he leaps (review fix M2).
- **The split lands on the music's drop.** He leaps on the music's breath and lands on its drop. The board cracks, **the big board is never drawn behind the four**, and the arcs strike from his hands at his sides. His second line comes after the landing; then the book returns, his leap home and "PHASE 2".
- **No God King absorption.** The God King stays greyed in his place throughout. Removed: `absorbKing`, `AbsorbStream`, the God King's triumph and drained lines (`foeTriumph`, `foeDrained`), `seizeTalk`, `absorb`, `revive`, `recluseDrain`, and the dock's absorption states and their CSS.
- **His suit and helmet replace the engine eye everywhere** his phase 2 look shows: his figure by the board, the phone bar's bust, his figure on the board and the results. It's done by swapping parts (`suitName`, `suitFrame`, `suited` in `characters/recluse.ts`), not an overlay. `EngineOverlay`, `placeEngine` and `setEngineAt` became `setSuitAt`.
- **Music:** the transformation theme's intro now starts 0.3 s into the phase change (a setting, `musicAtMs`). The e2e caught a device a few ms late falling back to "loop only"; the split is still on the drop.
- **Server and protocol:** nothing changed in the protocol. Two settings follow the screens: `RECLUSE_ONLINE.changeMs` goes from 34,300 to 23,700, and `RECLUSE_CUTSCENE.holdMs` from 111,120 to 107,480.
- **Code:** new `characters/recluse-transform.ts` and `components/RecluseTransform.tsx` (added to `e2e/areas.json`). The mockup lab and its preview script are removed. `scripts/frames-recluse-cutscene.mjs` now records the whole phase change with a still per beat and taps the mate.

## Review fixes (review on `review/707`)

- **B1, the phone frame budget, with the light's look kept** (Eric: "if the light looks worse quality I'll be upset"). The light was two screen-size canvases at the phone's full 3 device pixels per CSS pixel, redrawn every frame on the main thread. Now (`components/RecluseTransform.tsx`, `recluse-light.ts`, `recluse-light.worker.ts`):
  - **Drawn in a worker, off the main thread.** The two canvases go to a worker once (`transferControlToOffscreen`). Each frame the main thread sends where he and the book are; the worker draws only the latest frame. Where a browser can't hand a canvas to a worker, the main thread draws it as before. The drawing code is unchanged, moved into `recluse-light.ts` without the document.
  - **At 1 device pixel per CSS pixel** (the director's floor). In the worker that keeps up with the page's frames: 59 draws a second once the drawing-ahead is done. At the phone's full 3 it fell to 11 to 33 a second at the light's peak on the test machine.
  - **Profiling found the cost:** the canvases' pixels (Chrome's `DoUpdateLayers`). A micro-benchmark showed it is pure area (about 30 ns a pixel on the slowed main thread, rotated or not, `lighter` or not). The same drawing takes 9 ms in a worker against 39 ms on the slowed main thread.
  - **Each canvas is hidden, and not drawn, while it has nothing to draw** (`canvasesOn`). The back one is hidden under the full white; the front one from the reveal to the landing.
  - **Tried and dropped:** WebGL (same calls on the GPU) was no faster in the test's software-only browser (16% dropped at 1, 31% at 3). A third or half of a pixel per CSS pixel failed Eric's rule (below). CSS opacity layers for the full-screen dark and the white-out changed the look: the light adds onto the dark in the same canvas, which hides the board under the rays. Back in the canvas as before, they cost nothing extra.
  - **Why it looks the same:** the same sprites, transforms, alphas and adding, in the same canvases. The only change is pixels per CSS pixel, 1 instead of 3, for light that is soft and stretched anyway (its sprites hold under one pixel of detail per CSS pixel). Checked at the phone's own pixels (DPR 3) against the PR head, at exactly the same instants (Playwright's fake clock), at four beats: the light taking him, the rays at full, the white-out and dying back to the reveal. On average the frames differ by under half a level in 255 (1.5 during the white-out). At 1:1, the only visible difference is the tiny motes, a touch softer. The sheet is `light-before-after.png` on `stills/recluse-transform-book`.
  - **Also:** `lightNow` is computed once a frame, and `openMs` once.
- **M1.** The FAQ: "he transforms (the God King stays in his place, but can't be called for the rest of the fight)".
- **M2, his first line readable long enough.** Both (a) and (c): this line alone types at 30 ms a character (`chantLines.firstTypeMs`, a setting), and its box stays up after the visor comes down, through the crouch, until he leaps (`upAt`). **Readable whole: 2.8 s** (2,835 ms; it was 1,185 ms). His box isn't remounted between the visor and the crouch (one key while he stands), so it doesn't vanish mid-fade.
