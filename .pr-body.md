## Sign in without the age question; Sign up asks it

Eric, Nov 12: "You should be able to go to the site on a fresh browser and log in without having to select an age if you already
have an account... put a sign in already [first], and then below that... a sign up button, and then... your age thing."

**Full lane:** sign-in, accounts and the age gate. It needs the `full-e2e` label and an independent review. **No schema change, no
migration:** it uses `users.age_ok_at`, which already exists.

### What changed

- **Server** (`api.ts`, `accounts.ts`): the email start and Google's start no longer need an answer. A device that's too young is
  still refused at every step. The server decides only once it knows who is signing in (the code checked, or Google's
  callback):
  - this device just answered "old enough": signed in as before (the account made if needed, `age_ok_at` set, the answer used
    up);
  - no answer, and the account has answered (`age_ok_at` set): signed in, nothing about the age touched;
  - otherwise (no account yet, or an account from before the question): **asked first**. No session, and nothing made, moved or
    written. Email: 403 `{askAge, message: AGE_STEP_LINE}`, and the code stays live (`verifyEmailCode(…, keep)`). Google:
    `?signin=age-google`, with the trip's state spent.
  - New in `accounts.ts`: `signInIfAnswered` (the guarded sign-in) and `answeredSignIn` (the same rule, read only).
    `signInWithIdentity` is unchanged for its other callers.
- **App** (`SignIn.tsx`, `account.ts`, `Landing.tsx`, `App.tsx`, `styles.css`): one box with a Sign in view and a Sign up view.
  A sign-in waiting for the answer (`pending`) is finished by the answer:
  - email: the same code, sent again;
  - Google: one more trip to Google.
- **Docs:** decision `docs/decisions/2026-11-12-signin-without-age.md` (the calls and the attacker cases), `docs/areas/accounts.md`,
  `hub.md`, the privacy policy's "Children" (`Legal.tsx`), and `TESTING.md` (Eric's phone check: Google from a fresh private
  tab).

### What players see

- The front door: **Sign in to play online** with Continue with Google and the email code at once, no question. Below it:
  "New to HunChess?" **Sign up**.
- Sign up: "Sign up: when were you born?" in the same box, then the same two ways. The code's button says "Sign up". "Have an
  account? Sign in" goes back. Switching moves nothing below: both views are the same height (measured at 320, 390 and 428 px
  wide and at 1280, 1440 and 1920).
- Signing in with an email or Google account that has no account yet, or an account from before Oct 16: "One more step: when
  were you born?".
  - Email: nothing to type again.
  - Google: "Then pick your Google account again."
- Too young: the friendly line only, with no Sign in or Sign up (as before).
- Settings and the profile's sign-in boxes get the same two views, headed "Sign in" / "Sign up".
- Fixed: at 320 px the email box pushed the sign-in box 8 px past its column.

### What the player repeats

- **Email:** nothing. The code stays live (still 5 tries, 10 minutes, one finish).
- **Google:** one more pick of the account, only when the server must ask (a Google account with no account, or one from before
  the question). Finishing without it would mean keeping the Google identity of someone not yet asked, in a new table or a
  signed cookie (a new secret). With the second trip, nothing is kept before the answer. The decision explains this.

### Attacker cases

These are tested in `packages/server/test/age-gate.test.ts`, listed in the decision:

- a fresh browser;
- an unknown email, and an unknown Google account (nothing written, not even a cookie naming the account);
- an account from before the question;
- a too-young cookie with an answered, a pre-gate or no account, at every step, including a Google state from before the
  answer and a code got elsewhere;
- the answer running out between steps;
- two tabs (one uses up the other's answer; two finishes at once give one sign-in and one account);
- a replayed callback (on another device, or after the trip that asked);
- the kept code's limits;
- a wrong code answering the same for known and unknown emails (no probing for accounts).
