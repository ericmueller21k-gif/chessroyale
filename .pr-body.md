## What changed

The judge now scores a crowd move that sets off Sawyer's Saw Swap on the board **after** the swap, him to move (Eric, Nov 21: "Yeah, that sounds good"). This closes the gap the review of #856 found (its decision's call 5): a checking fork used to be scored as winning the rook, though the swap rearranges his half and usually breaks the fork.

- **Which moves:** while the swap is still to come, every allowed crowd move that would set it off as it lands: a check with his king on his half where `sawSwapFen` is legal. Never a mate (the mate stands), a draw on the board, the battle's last move (the move cap), a tower floor with `sawSwapInTower` off, or a side of several bosses. One predicate (`sawyerBattle.swapToCome`) now serves both the swap itself and the judge (`sawyerBattle.judgeSwaps`, through a new `BossRules.judgeAfter` hook). Once the swap is used, nothing changes.
- **How it's scored:** the board after the swap gets the same top-moves search every position gets (`TopMovesCache`, prefetched while players think). The move's score is 1 minus his best line there (`swapScores`). The runner folds the scores in once, in `resolveBoard` (`swapJudged`), and only for moves it finds set off the swap itself, so a host can't move another move's score. The best move, every move's loss, the bots' picks, solo's hints and eval bar, the meter's judged number and the God King's Last Stand all follow from that. For such a move the Last Stand names no reply (the plain board's reply means nothing once the swap happens).
- **Online:** the lobby sends the moves and their boards in the job (`swap` on `ScoreJob`, `JudgeJob` and the prefetch plan) and the boards in the prefetch's `fens`. The host and each judge search them and report the crowd's side (`swapped` on `BoardScore`, `swap` on `JudgeReport`). Two judges' looks must match exactly (`boardsAgree`). A report with the wrong moves or numbers doesn't hold together. An older app's report without them is read as plain chess (no strike). The engine server never sees the swapped boards: the re-check and its verdict stay plain chess, and a verdict keeps only the looks both judges give alike (`sharedSwaps`). Solo and online run the same code.
- **Fair play:** no code change. The runner's existing rule for a boss's judge that changes the numbers (written for the Carrot Patch's race) applies: the turn counts only when the engine's own best keeps its score, and fair play then records the engine's own numbers.
- **Not changed:** online hints (they don't read boss rules today, the fire's neither).

Real Stockfish, a bare fork (`r3k3/8/8/1N6/8/8/8/6K1 w`, Nb5-c7+ against Ke8 and Ra8): plain chess scored it 0.50 (it wins the rook, then a dead draw). On the board after the swap (`k3r3/6N1/8/8/8/8/8/6K1 b`) the crowd is lost, 0.00: the knight on g7 is trapped by the rook.
