## The recipe that worked: write the next themes this way

Eric on Moo & Moon's third go (song_r.py, Nov 12): "please whatever ... u did for this do it for the rest, this is pure
magic, i could listen to this anytime" (he meant: keep it in the notes for the bosses still to come). What that song
does, in the order it was built:

1. **The boss first.** Read his row in `docs/areas/bosses.md` and his sounds file in `packages/app/src/characters/`.
   The lead is the boss; a second voice is his sidekick, in small doses (a few words in the gaps, never a section of
   its own); two or three sounds from his world, built as the game builds them and kept quiet.
2. **Tunes off the grid.** Triplets across the beat, octave jumps that float back down, notes held over the bar line,
   phrases of three bars beside the usual two and four, a lead that slides into its long notes. Not: 8ths on the grid,
   two-bar question and answer, the same shape moved from chord to chord.
3. **A chord a bar**, the bass stepping (by half steps there) under one note that never moves and belongs to every
   chord; a chord from outside the key before the turn home.
4. **A second tune that shifts in the bar:** a motif of an odd length (five 8ths), so it lands somewhere new each
   time, over chords moving by whole steps.
5. **The last tune a minor third up**, and a short tag that walks home for the loop.
6. **Two calms of about 15 seconds:** no drums, slow strings on a chord with a raised fourth, the fast pattern at half
   speed, something of the boss's world (wind chimes for stargazing, stopped two bars early so none hangs), a flute; the
   lead playing slowly in one of them.
7. **One drop:** the lead and a second lead trade two bars each, then play together; chords a major third apart; an
   808, a wobble bass, a stutter at the end of each turn.
8. **The fast pattern is from his world** (stars) and keeps coming back to the note that never moves.
9. **Measure before sending:** each voice's level section by section, the spectrum against an approved song, the calm
   about 6 dB under the rest. Then send, and expect two or three rounds: the first go was "too slow ... too deep", the
   second "feels like the other ones a lot".

Vary the particulars from boss to boss (which way the bass steps, how long the shifting motif is, what the calm is
made of): the point is the kind of move, not these notes.

Boingo (song_s.py, Nov 12) followed it and went a step further, as Eric asked ("how you kind of went adventurous, this
one, even take it up another notch"): the bass steps up instead of down, the motif is seven 8ths, the calms are a
broken music box and a whistle three half steps flat, the drop is the tune upside down, and one idea from the boss's
world shapes the whole song (a merry-go-round whose speed changes). His verdict on the first go, with no changes
asked: "This is flawless and your best work yet." So for the next bosses: the recipe, plus one such idea from the
boss's world that reaches into how the song itself runs, not only its sounds.

## What another pair of ears heard (Nov 13)

Eric had another listener describe song AA and sent its notes: the only description there is of how these synthesized
voices come across, so check a new song against it. A kick on 1 and 3 with extra hits and open hats off the beat read
as "four-on-the-floor ... synth-disco"; the slap bass (FM, thumbed and popped) as "a dry, mechanical 16th-note
synth-bass"; `Mix.drive` as "a polite, clean synth-brass/sawtooth patch"; a banjo's roll (short picked notes in
16ths) as "shimmering 16th-note Euro-synthpop arpeggios"; the picked-string twang as "harpsichord/bell-like"; a
strings pad as "polite synth-pads"; long slides from note to note as "a wobbly, drunken ambulance siren"; runs of
16ths as "frantic ... scale runs". Its rules for a rock or Western song, which he passed on: no kick on every beat
(1 and 3, pickups, a heavy snare on 2 and 4); no runs of 16ths up and down a scale, no cheerful arpeggios, rests
between phrases; a guitar's bend goes up one or two half steps into the note and is held with vibrato, never a slow
wide slide; a galloping bass (an 8th and two 16ths); a dry strummed guitar, not pads. song_ac.py is built to them:
its distorted guitar is clipped hard and put through a speaker's curve (`axe`, `speaker`).

## What he asks of every theme

Later, not now (Nov 13): "at some point ... we'll need to maybe create midis of them ... just something to keep in
your notes." Every song's notes are already lists in its file (the lines' `name:beats` text, the bass and kit
patterns), so a MIDI file of a theme is a small script over those, not a transcription.

A theme from a write-up of a style (Nov 13: Dash's and Puddles's, each from a write-up made from another game's
track): "take these notes and make your own version. Same rules as always." Use the style's means (the pace, the
swing, the kinds of voices, how dry or echoing it is, how the parts contrast); never the write-up's chords, riffs or
tune, nor its "original" version in another key (that is the track's chords moved); don't listen to the track. Say
in the song's docstring and to Eric which was which. Both passed first go.

Two minutes from the start (or a little more when the intro wants it: Nov 12, "it's our game ... let's just make it a
little bit longer than 2 minutes and it'll still loop"; for the Hidden Boss, "if this song is 2:30-3 that's fine"),
loop-clean, an intro of its own; a fast pattern underneath; a switch every eight bars or
so (he liked Big Boy's variety most); instruments layered and trading in different parts; a slower, simpler stretch
somewhere; and where it fits, a drop. When he doesn't like a lead, send him short samples of the same tune on other
instruments: he picks fast.

A big middle (Nov 12, the Hidden Boss's): when the main part doesn't hit, he asks for it faster and wilder with a new
voice in front: "that hymning ... really epic ... a holy sensation, but ... a lot of instrumentals going around with
it, a lot of fast-pacedness" (a wordless men's choir over a racing band). A build that pays off wants a faster part
after it, not a slower one.

A longer song for a two-part boss (Nov 12, the Hidden Boss's): after the first big part, a quiet memory (a place,
its background sounds, a soft warm tune) that builds, goes out of sync and out of key, and screeches; a slight pause;
the big part again with more on it ("a bit more layers on top ... kind of pause ... long note, short note"); the
ending as before. "It doesn't matter how much you add."

Tried on that memory and not chosen (Nov 12; none of them disliked, he just picked others): for the tune, a harp with
the choir as children (its first go: "that one's not bad"), a breathy flute, a pan flute, a wet finger on a glass's
rim, a celesta; for the rolling chords, the harp, a soft-mallet marimba, a vibraphone, a small organ's flutes.

A score played in pieces, by a boss's phase (Nov 12 and 13, the Hidden Boss: phases_v.py has his words and the layout):
the house's loop while he is a child; the score's first half for the first fight; the childhood music again only
when he's beaten and talks; the souring while he transforms, the big part dropping when the boards split; then the
second half looping, the childhood music never again. "We don't know how long the games run ... every part should
be self-contained, but also be able to be strung into the next part." So each point of the fight is a theme of the
usual shape (an intro or none, and a loop) and the game only switches between them; the one length that has to
match an animation is the souring's. Each piece is made by itself so no echo is cut, and the score's pieces take the
whole score's gain. Handed over Nov 13 when he asked what the director needs to wire it (`boss-music/` on
`stills/trailer-v2`).
The same day the session wiring it sent the transformation's real length and beats (8.74 s from his rising to the
boards' crack, the book landing 2.64 s in), so the game's souring has its own layout (song_v.py's `RAGE`): the tune's
three notes once, sour; a hush; the book; the spiral's last units as the build; a breath; the drop. The score's own
souring is untouched. He heard it and asked for two things: about five seconds more on the picture "so we get the
souring longer" (the build's length is `RAGE`'s first number; the longer cut ships when the animation is lengthened),
and the pause before the drop "longer like a full second it will make it more epic" (it was a fifth of one: a tick,
then the sound dying to nothing).
He then chose two lines for the man to say in the chant ("Computation without creativity is nothing. Now I have
both." "Prepare yourself. This is the ultimate test of your skill."); the director built them (17.435 s from his
rising to the crack, the last line typed as the silence begins) and the cut was fitted to that: `RAGE` (13.395, 6,
1.0), its build the spiral's last six units, the first with only the sour notes.

A place's own loop from a song's quiet part (Nov 12, the Hidden Boss's house as a child: song_w.py): "just that soft,
cozy thing ... like a 1-minute loop that doesn't do any of the crazy like spiraling stuff ... nice and cozy". The
same tune, chords and instruments by themselves, the second half turned over (another instrument takes the tune, a
new answering phrase) so the minute isn't thirty seconds twice, and the place's own small sounds, quiet.

Into a drop (Nov 12, Ginger's): more of the build's own sound right before it ("a little more like ice crack"), then
"a little bit of ... pause with ... quiet, like jingling", "and then all of a sudden like the big ice shatter, and
then it jumps back into it": a hush of a bar, one big hit from the boss's world with room to be heard, then the drop.
